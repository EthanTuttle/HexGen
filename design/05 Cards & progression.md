# HexGen — Cards & progression

Last updated Oct 27, 2026 · @Ethan · Part of the [Index](00 Index.md)

Map card types, the deck and hand, how the hand refills, deck shaping, levels and milestones, general feats, starting decks, class locations and their quests, and the landmark list. Where and how cards are placed on the board: [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Placement.

## Card types

Decided Oct 23–24, 2026 (the rarity rethink). The old Common/Rare/Legendary map card tiers are retired. Once placing became Dorfromantik-style mass building, Rare cards felt like just more landmarks, since regions already create locations on their own. Now regions make every landmark, and the deck holds only terrain.

- **Terrain cards:** the only cards in the deck and hand. Each is one Common terrain type (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Exploration and terrain). Some have multi-hex footprints (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Placement). Corrupted versions can enter the deck (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Corruption).
- **Story cards:** found through the main quest line. They never go to the hand: the story places them at fixed locations, so players have to travel to reach them. Starting hexes and all story locations (e.g. Gronogon, the cultist dungeon, the second wizard tower, the highest peak, the prison dungeon) are Story cards. (How the story places them: [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Placement.)
- **Landmarks are not cards.** Players never place landmarks. A finished region converts one random tile into a landmark rolled from its region variant's pool (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Clustered regions). The former Rare cards now live in those pools (see Landmark list).
- **Legendaries** are mostly the locations of rare tier 3 region variants, which a quest can guarantee; the Sword in the Stone is placed by a quest (see Landmark list and [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Tier 3 variants). How each quest starts: see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Quests.
- Gear still has rarity tiers (see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Equipment).

## Deck and hand

- The deck is a terrain supply, not a deck builder. The Dominion-style "start plain, build up with interesting cards" intent was dropped on Oct 23, 2026; the interesting part lives on the map.
- Terminology: the deck and the draw pool are the same thing (the player's map-card deck). The loot pool is separate and holds gear and items.
- Starting map deck: 15 terrain cards (17 for clerics), set by class, background and god (see Starting decks).
- The deck grows through level milestones' terrain picks (see Levels and milestones).
- Hand limit is 7 cards (cut from 10 on Oct 24, 2026, so each placement weighs more). **The limit is checked at rest** (decided Oct 24, 2026): draws can take the hand past 7 while exploring and the player keeps building; when the party rests, the player discards down to 7. (Before: discard down as soon as the hand went over, which stopped building until the player discarded.) Discarded cards go to a discard pile, which is reshuffled into the deck only by resting (see Empty deck). The Big Hand feat raises the limit by 1 (see General feats).
- Players can view every card they own, but not the draw order (exception: the Surveyor feat; see General feats).
- How map cards and combat action cards look different: see [Presentation](07 Presentation.md).

## Core loop and hand refill

- Core loop: play cards from hand to build regions; finishing regions earns more cards to keep going; once the player runs out and can't make more patterns or groups, they go out exploring with their hero to refresh their hand.
- Placing a card never draws a new one. The scarcity is in choosing which card to place where.
- Cards are drawn from finishing regions, winning fights, completing quests and story beats. Night fights draw extra cards (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Day and night). Resting draws nothing: it reshuffles the discard pile into the deck and enforces the hand limit (see Deck and hand).
- Draw amounts scale with difficulty so one fight gets the player back to building: an easy fight or tier 1 region draws 3; a tough fight, quest or tier 2 region draws 5; a boss or Legendary quest refills the hand to the limit (7).
- **Empty deck:** if the deck runs out before a rest, any draw from a reward is lost. The player has to rest to reshuffle the discard pile first, so rest timing matters.

## Deck shaping

Instead of plain deck thinning (a smaller deck runs dry sooner and loses building material):

- Wizard towers (a landmark) swap a card for one of a different terrain, keeping the deck the same size, so players steer which terrains they draw.
- Druid enclaves (a landmark) cleanse corrupted cards in the deck, restoring each to its normal version (matching how churches and temples cleanse corrupted hexes; see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Corruption).
- Plain land cards can't simply be removed.

## Levels and milestones

- Level cap: 100.
- **Working scale, not final** (noted Oct 22, 2026): the 1–100 spread leaves room between levels to add things while designing. A level condense is planned before release, and in the final game every level-up should grant something; a level with no reward feels bad. Level numbers in these docs (e.g. 5, 10, 15, 20) are placeholders for that condense.
- Every 5 levels, a milestone offers terrain picks: the player chooses terrain cards to add to the deck permanently (changed Oct 24, 2026 from a rarity-weighted pick of 1 card from 3). How many cards and choices: open.
- **Ability score increases at levels 8 and 16** (restored Oct 27, 2026; an Oct 26 change had replaced them with ability points on every level-up): each character gets +1 ability point, which the player places on the score they choose. Other levels give no ability points.
- **General feats** at levels 4 and 12 (decided Oct 27, 2026): see General feats below.
- Level milestones also widen sight range (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Day and night).
- Class card unlocks by level (class action cards, spells): see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Class action cards. New combat cards come only from level-ups (exceptions: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Class action cards); class locations upgrade them and teach skills (decided Oct 22, 2026; see Class locations).
- **Weapon mastery trainings by level 15** (decided Oct 22, 2026): Fighters 6 (enough to finish a whole tree), Rogues and Rangers 3 (one full branch). Which levels they unlock at: progression pass. Leader bards keep their 3 at levels 10, 15 and 20 (see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Weapon mastery). Feat levels 4 and 12 leave 2, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14 free before level 15, enough for the Fighter's 6.

### General feats

Decided Oct 27, 2026. Feats are perks any class can pick at set levels.

- **Levels:** 4 and 12 (placeholders for the level condense). Neither level grants another unlock. Feats after level 20: open.
- **Requirements:** total level, an ability score, race or background. Never class.
- **Picking:** a free pick from every feat the character qualifies for. Level-12 feats need level 12, and the level-12 pick can also be any level-4 feat.
- **What a feat can do:** mostly passive perks; a few grant an action card (new combat cards come only from level-ups, and a feat pick is a level-up). Feats build only on rules that already exist.
- **Companions:** every companion gets feats, at the same levels and with the same free pick as the hero, story NPC companions included.
- **Stacking:** a feat stacks with a race trait or proficiency effect that does the same thing (e.g. Tough with the shield dwarf's extra health; Keen Eye with Perception's +1 sight range, for +2). Race traits: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Races; proficiencies: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Skills.
- Score requirements (2+ for level-4 feats, 3+ for level-12 feats) are placeholders. Scores start at 1 to 3 (see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Ability scores → Starting scores), so a 3+ requirement can already be met at level 1.
- Names stay short and plain, especially for early feats.

## Starting decks

Decided Oct 24, 2026 (replaces class map decks with 1–2 class-flavored Rare starters). The starting deck is lean only, with no even base mix, so level-up terrain picks matter more.

- **Class:** 2 terrains, 5 cards each. The first is the class quest terrain (see Class quests).
- **Background:** 1 terrain, 5 cards (backgrounds: see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Backgrounds).
- **Cleric god:** +2 cards of the god's terrain, so clerics start with 17. Holy Defenders add their god's 2 cards when they pick the god at level 5.
- That makes 15 cards (17 for clerics), usually covering 3 terrains.
- **Overlap:** if the background's terrain matches a class terrain, the cards stack. 5 of the 36 class + background combos overlap: Soldier + Wizard (ruins), Acolyte + Wizard (hills), Criminal + Cleric (river/lake), Sage + Fighter (desert), Folk Hero + Ranger (forest). Folk Hero + Ranger is kept on purpose as a forest-focused specialist. The other likely pairings (Soldier + Fighter, Acolyte + Cleric, Criminal + Rogue, Sage + Wizard, Noble + Bard) don't overlap.
- **Pairing check:** every combo that mixes banned pairings (e.g. desert with forest, farmland with swamp) also has a terrain that can sit next to both, so no starting deck is boxed in.

## Class locations

Each class has a class location, grown from its terrain's class variant (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Region variants and locations). Class locations are open to everyone for a general service, plus special services (including subclass change) for their own class.

| Class | Class location | Grows from (class variant) | Own-class services |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fighter | Training Grounds | Plains (Tourney fields) | Fighters learn skills (Athletics, Perception, Intimidate, Survival), upgrade subclass cards and change subclass |
| Rogue | Thieves Guild | Swamp (Smugglers' bayou) | Rogues learn skills (Stealth, Thievery, Deceive, Intimidate), upgrade subclass cards and change subclass |
| Wizard | Library | Hills/mountains (Monastery heights) | Learn skills (Lore, Nature, Perception, Persuade), change wizard subclass; a spell pick from your school at each visit (separate from the Legendary ancient library) |
| Cleric | Meditation Rock | River/lake (Still waters) | Attempt to commune with their god, learn skills (Lore, Persuade, Perception, Survival), pick a spell from their order at each visit, change cleric order |
| Ranger | Tree of Life | Forest (Heartwood) | Rangers commune with nature to learn skills (Survival, Nature, Animal and Perception proficiencies) and change subclass. (Nature cards such as calling vines or animal spirits moved to Ranger level-ups, Oct 22, 2026) |
| Bard | College | Village (Scholars' town) | Swap their instrument, learn flourishes (skills), change bard subclass |

- **Guarantee** (decided Oct 24, 2026): each class is guaranteed its own class location through its level-5 class quest (see Class quests). Any class can still roll any class variant normally. Half-classes also get the borrowed class's quest (see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Half-classes).
- **Locations upgrade, level-ups grant** (decided Oct 22, 2026): for its own class, a class location upgrades the character's subclass cards and teaches skills. It doesn't upgrade class cards (Second Wind, Hunter's Mark, Snare and the like), since subclasses already upgrade those at level 5. It doesn't teach new action cards or spells, which come only from level-ups. Exceptions: Holy Defenders (and clerics) still unlock their later god spell through meditations at Meditation Rock, since that grows the god bond; and weapon mastery nodes that add a new weapon card are still learned at Training Grounds, since mastery is improving the weapon. **Visits** (decided Oct 22, 2026, the template for every class location): one unlock per visit, gated by level (levels 10 and 20 so far; later gates in the progression pass). Rhythm: each 5-level step brings one thing, a new subclass card from a level-up (5, 15) or an upgrade to the previous one at the location (10, 20), so a new card is never upgraded the moment you get it (decided Oct 22, 2026; the location upgrade moved from 15 to 20). Each unlock gives both 1 skill and 1 subclass-card upgrade, so out-of-combat skills never cost combat power. The skill is a pick of 1 from the location's skills (e.g. the Tree of Life offers Survival, Nature, Animal and Perception; martial locations offer 4 each; the Human Quick Study feat makes it 2: see General feats). **Casters** (decided Oct 22, 2026): casters gain complexity through spell choices and martials through upgrades, so a caster location's visit gives a spell pick from the character's subclass school instead of an upgrade (an exception to "locations don't teach new cards"). Wizard and Cleric tiers: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Wizard spells and Cleric spells; Bard: in progress. What each location upgrades: in progress.
- **General services:** Tree of Life restores health (a future Druid class would learn exclusively there). Training Grounds lets all martials train weapon mastery (see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Weapon mastery). Thieves Guild is a cheap shop. Library: everyone can learn lore and identify items. Meditation Rock: everyone can receive a short blessing buff for their next fight. College: anyone can learn an instrument for gold, or swap the one they know; instruments are just for fun, played in combat with no effect (rules: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Instruments; decided Oct 25, 2026).
- Skills taught at class locations: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Skills.
- The old random tile conversion into class locations (e.g. Training Grounds from village or desert) was removed on Oct 24, 2026: class locations come only from class variant regions.

### Class quests (level 5)

At level 5, a side quest starts automatically. **Build first, then the task** (decided Oct 24, 2026): the quest asks the hero to build a region of the class's terrain. That region's roll is guaranteed to be the class variant, so it grows the class location. Building it reveals the quest site inside the region, where the hero completes a class-relevant task. One class quest per class:

| Class | Builds | Quest site | Task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fighter | Plains → Tourney fields | Arena | Win a gauntlet of fights |
| Rogue | Swamp → Smugglers' bayou | Noble's manor | Pull off a heist |
| Wizard | Hills/mountains → Monastery heights | Ruined archive | Recover a lost spellbook |
| Cleric | River/lake → Still waters | Desecrated shrine of their god | Cleanse it |
| Ranger | Forest → Heartwood | Sick old forest | Track and slay what's killing it |
| Bard | Village → Scholars' town | Festival | Win a performance contest |

What the task itself rewards, now that the location already exists once the region is built: open.

The gear items themselves are TBD. Locations that reuse existing landmarks (wizard tower, ancient library, beast's lair/den) are Story-specific variants, like the class quest sites.
