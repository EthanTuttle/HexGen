# HexGen — Classes & combat

Last updated Oct 27, 2026 · @Ethan · Part of the [Index](00 Index.md)

Heroes and companions, races, ability scores, coin checks, tactical combat, classes and subclasses, class action cards and all spell schools. Weapons, weapon cards and mastery trees live in [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md).

## Hero and companions

- The player controls a hero plus companions. Up to 3 companions at once.
- Companions are gained by recruiting in locations and as quest rewards.
- Companions have full classes and gear, like the hero, and level up with the hero.
- In tactical combat, the player controls companions directly.
- Story companions (path NPCs) and their restrictions: see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Background paths.

## Hero creation

The hero is defined by class, race/ancestry and background. All three use custom lists. Backgrounds are in [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Backgrounds.

- 6 classes at launch: Fighter, Rogue, Wizard, Cleric, Ranger, Bard.
- No multiclassing; heroes choose a subclass at level 5. Each class has 4 subclasses.
- Classes play mostly as in D&D. Ranger brings bonuses to exploration (crossing terrain faster) and ranged combat; Bard brings bonuses to social encounters and support tools.
- Starting ability scores: see Ability scores → Starting scores.

## Ability scores

- D&D's six: STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA.
- **Starting scores** (decided Oct 27, 2026): every score starts at 1, so every character can try every check. Then:
  - **Race:** +1 to 2 of the race's 3 boosted scores (the player picks; see Races).
  - **Class:** +1 to one of the class's 3 main scores (the player picks; table below).
  - If the class +1 lands on a score the race also boosted, that score starts at 3 (one 3, one 2, four 1s). If not, three scores start at 2 (three 2s, three 1s).
- Scores grow at levels 8 and 16 (+1, placed by the player: see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Levels and milestones), from gear, and from cards/discoveries.

| Class | Main scores (the class +1 picks from these) |
| --- | --- |
| Fighter | STR, DEX, CON |
| Rogue | DEX, INT, CHA |
| Wizard | INT, CON, DEX |
| Cleric | WIS, STR, CON |
| Ranger | DEX, CON, WIS |
| Bard | CHA, DEX, STR |

## Coin checks

- Each check flips coins; heads count toward success.
- A check passes when it reaches its heads target. At low levels the target is mostly 1; heads targets rise at higher levels so coin counts stay manageable.
- Ability scores set how many coins are flipped on checks and attacks, and decide damage. A score of 3 flips 3 coins; scores start at 1 to 3 (see Ability scores → Starting scores).
- Skills/proficiency, gear and class also affect the number of coins.
- Coins can be modified with rerolls/reflips and weighted or special coins.
- How coins look: see [Presentation](07 Presentation.md).
- **Pass chances** (maths reference, Oct 27, 2026): with score increases alone a main score that starts at 3 goes 3 → 4 (level 8) → 5 (level 16), so raised heads targets only work if gear and proficiency add coins too.

| Coins | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Needs 1 heads | 50% | 75% | 88% | 94% | 97% | 98% | 99% | ~100% | ~100% |
| Needs 2 heads | — | 25% | 50% | 69% | 81% | 89% | 94% | 96% | 98% |
| Needs 3 heads | — | — | 13% | 31% | 50% | 66% | 77% | 86% | 91% |

- **Success checks:** weapon attacks flip coins to hit. For spells (decided Oct 23, 2026): single-target damage spells flip to hit, using the caster's coins; area and multi-target damage, debuffs and control make each target flip coins to save, using the ability the spell targets; summons, buffs and self-effects have no roll (a summon's own attacks flip to hit). Exceptions: single-target spells that strike the mind or soul (psychic, holy flame, necrotic: e.g. Sacred Flame, Toll the Dead, Finger of Death, Mockery, Psychic Spike) use a save; spells that are weapon attacks (Dancer and Leader cards) always flip, per target; ground zones (Sunspot, Thorn Patch) have no roll. The caster's casting ability makes the save harder (which ability: see each class's spell section; exact numbers TBD). **Spell scaling** (decided Oct 23, 2026): spell damage, healing and save difficulty grow with the caster's casting ability (INT for wizards, WIS for clerics, CHA for bards), which already grows on level-up and from gear, the same way weapon attacks grow with STR or DEX. Spells themselves don't get upgrades.

## Checks outside combat

Decided Oct 22, 2026. Details for each kind of check are in progress.

- **Kinds of checks:** social (persuade, deceive, intimidate in dialogue encounters), traversal (climbing, swimming, crossing hazards), dungeon and hex events (traps, locks, hidden doors, puzzles) and scouting (spotting, tracking, revealing nearby hexes).
- **Checks and combat:** before a fight, a check can decide who is surprised (surprised foes count as unaware, e.g. for Sneak Attack and Assassinate), and a stealth or social check can avoid an encounter entirely.
- **Who flips:** depends on the check. Some are group checks (everyone flips, e.g. sneaking past); others are solo checks, made by one party member.
- **Failure:** each check sets its own cost of failure.
- **Scouting:** two checks. Spot encounters: entering a hex, a check reveals encounters on nearby hexes and what they are. Ambush: before a fight, a check decides who is surprised.
- **Avoiding a fight:** an avoided encounter isn't cleared. It stays on the map until it despawns as normal (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Hex contents and encounters), so it can still be fought later.
- **Social:** three approaches, Persuade, Deceive and Intimidate, each its own proficiency. Dialogue options that need a check are tagged with an approach and use that proficiency's base ability (see Skills); not every option needs a check (decided Oct 28, 2026), and untagged ones just talk. The hero always does the talking.
- **Encounter scenes can offer any check** (decided Oct 28, 2026): a scene's options aren't limited to Persuade, Deceive and Intimidate. Any check fits where the scene calls for it: a proficiency check (e.g. Lore, Athletics) or a bare ability check with no proficiency (e.g. "[STR] Force the sword out"). The same zoomed-hex scene is used for any encounter with choices, such as the sword pull (see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Sword in the Stone). Social options are always the hero's. **The player picks who tries any other check** (chosen Oct 28, 2026, from social encounter v2a): players who want to lean on their companions can, and players who want to do everything with the hero can too. Not picked: always the hero (v2b); auto-pick by highest score (v2c).
- **Companion chime-in** (decided Oct 28, 2026; widened the same day from social checks to any proficiency check): only when the one flipping misses by exactly 1 heads, a companion proficient in that check's proficiency steps in on their own, says one of their own chime-in lines and flips 1 extra coin; heads saves the check. Bare ability checks get no chime-in. **When several companions are proficient** (decided Oct 28, 2026), the one with the highest score for the check steps in; ties go by party order. **Silver Tongue** (the feat's reflip, [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → General feats; decided Oct 28, 2026) comes first and automatically: on a failed social check, the hero reflips; the companion steps in only if the reflip misses by exactly 1. An extra coin only changes the result when the flipper is 1 short, so the odds are the same as always adding it; saving it for the near miss makes it a clutch moment rather than a routine bonus. How it shows: [Presentation](07 Presentation.md) → Social encounter screen.
- **Failure in a scene** (decided Oct 28, 2026): each option sets its own cost, in line with Failure above. A gentle approach failing can cost little (the NPC just says no); a harsh one can cost a lot. A failed check can start a fight where the scene says so (the NPC fights back); whether an NPC fights back is set per scene.
- **Dungeon and hex events:** traps, locks, secrets and other event checks can be tried by any party member (the player picks who: see Encounter scenes above); proficiency makes them reliable (no class-only options). Where events and dungeons happen: see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Hex contents and encounters.
- **Traversal:** crossing obstacles (cliffs, deep water, dense growth, harsh climate); one party member leads the check, picked by the player. Rules: see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Obstacles.

## Skills

Skills (learned at class locations such as Training Grounds, Tree of Life and College; see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations) are a mix of proficiencies, which add coins to related checks, and class-flavored perks and card upgrades. Class locations don't teach new action cards (see Class action cards). The full skill list is TBD.

**Proficiency rules** (decided Oct 22, 2026)

- A proficiency adds 1 coin to the checks it covers, and the bonus grows later on (how and when: open).
- Each proficiency covers a kind of check and has a base ability: a check flips the base ability's coins plus the proficiency's.

| Proficiency | Base | Checks it covers | Signature effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Persuade | CHA | Persuade dialogue options | Haggle: better prices at shops and inns |
| Deceive | CHA | Lies and bluffs | Bluff past an encounter, or start the fight with foes unaware |
| Intimidate | STR | Threats | Shaken start: foes start the fight frightened (flip one fewer coin on their first attack) |
| Perception | WIS | Spotting encounters, ambush defense, hidden doors | +1 sight range (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Day and night), which also reveals encounters farther away |
| Stealth | DEX | Ambushing, sneaking past (group check) | Ambush: the party can ambush, so foes start the fight unaware |
| Athletics | STR | Cliffs and deep water | No gear needed: lead a climb or swim without crossing gear |
| Survival | CON | Dense growth and harsh climate | No gear needed: lead through dense growth or harsh climate without crossing gear (the rough-terrain version of Athletics) |
| Nature | INT | Terrain, plant and weather events | Herbalism: hex events with plants can yield a healing consumable |
| Animal | WIS | Beast encounters | Calm beasts: beast encounters can be calmed so they don't attack (they stay on the map) |
| Thievery | DEX | Locks, traps, sleight of hand | Grid traps too: can also disarm traps on the combat grid |
| Lore | INT | Riddles, arcane and religious events, puzzles | Hints: get hints on riddles and puzzles (e.g. Sandstone Sphinx, Troll Bridge) |

(Decided Oct 22, 2026. A proficiency's signature effect works on top of its coin bonus.)
- A proficiency can be learned again to raise it a rank (more coins). Rank cap: open.
- **Starting proficiencies** (decided Oct 28, 2026): a new hero starts with 2: one picked from their background's short list and one from their class location's list (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations). Story companions start with 2, hand-picked to fit who they are, regardless of class (see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Background paths).
- Sources: class locations (1 pick per visit from that location's list; see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations) and backgrounds (1 pick at creation from the background's short list; the lists: open).

## Turns, AP and health

- Every character gets 3 action points per turn.
- **Bonus AP** (from cards such as Action Surge, the Tactician's Action Surge upgrade or Crimson Pact) is spent before the normal 3, and any bonus AP left unspent expires at the end of the turn. **Cap** (decided Oct 22, 2026): no character can have more than 6 AP in a turn (3 base + 3 bonus, matching the combat screen); bonus AP past that is lost. Summons and the Companion's beast count as allies for party-wide effects (e.g. Crimson Pact, the Tactician's Action Surge upgrade), so they can gain bonus AP too, up to the same cap. How bonus AP is shown: see [Presentation](07 Presentation.md) → Combat screen.
- Characters spend action points to take actions. In the UI, actions are shown as cards.
- Action cards come from the character's class and their gear/weapons.
- Health is an HP pool.

## Combat

- Tactical grid fights happen by zooming into the hex where the fight starts.
- **Hex grid** (changed from squares, Oct 21, 2026): the tactical grid is made of small hexes. Every distance, move, reach and area in card text is counted in hexes, and each hex has 6 neighbours ("adjacent" means one of those 6).
- Turns go side by side: all allies act, in any order, then all enemies.
- Checks and combat are coin-based (see Coin checks).
- Combat action cards are always available: every learned action can be used each turn. A hotbar capped at 6 slots holds the player's quick picks, and a separate full-list menu holds every available action. Each action costs 1–3 action points, and there are no cooldowns, spell slots or mana (Ritualist blood arts also cost HP). A few cards are once per fight instead (marked where listed).
- **Once per turn by default** (decided Oct 21, 2026): weapon cards, class cards and spells can each be used once per turn. Move and the basic attack are repeatable; any other card that can be repeated is marked "repeatable" where it is listed. Once-per-fight cards (e.g. Second Wind, Action Surge) can be used once per combat (decided Oct 21, 2026). (Weapon cards: see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Mastery tree framework.)
- Every character has basic actions: move, basic attack with the equipped weapon, defend/brace, and use a consumable. Each costs 1 AP. Move and the basic attack can be repeated within a turn. One Move covers a fixed number of grid hexes, modified by DEX and race.
- A downed ally stays down until combat ends; healing cannot bring them back mid-fight. Exceptions: the Order of Good's Resurrection (once per fight), the Companion ranger's Mend Beast (beast only), and two that stop the downing instead: the Conley tiefling's From the Ashes (once per day cycle; see Races) and the Hard to Kill feat (once per fight; see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → General feats).
- **Unaware** (decided Oct 22, 2026): a status shown on the foe. A foe becomes unaware when it's ambushed at the start of a fight (Stealth or Deceive), or of a character who is hidden. It ends per attacker: once you attack an unaware foe, it's no longer unaware of you, but stays unaware of your allies. Used by Sneak Attack, Assassinate and Fake Surrender.
- **Darkness** (decided Oct 25, 2026): a hex is either lit or dark. Two kinds of dark:
  - **Natural night:** night fights (and dark places such as caves) are dark. Attacks against a target in natural night flip one fewer coin. Ranged builds still work at night.
  - **Magical darkness** (from spells): attacks against a target in it flip one fewer coin, and a creature in it can't target anything more than 1 hex away. It affects everyone equally unless the spell says otherwise; the Order of Night's spells affect foes only, like other divine magic.
  - Spells that care about "in darkness" (Shadow Bolt, Shadow Cloak, Snuff the Light) count both kinds.
  - **Counters** (decided Oct 26, 2026):
    - Light spells: any Order of Light or Aurora spell with an area (Dawn Pulse, Sunlit Ground, Lance of Dawn, Pillar of Light, Radiant Nova, Sunspot, Aurora's Wrath) clears magical darkness on the hexes it hits.
    - Fire: a fire spell lights the hexes it hits until the end of the round; then the darkness returns.
    - Light gear lights natural night on its holder's hex and the 6 around it: a torch is a cheap consumable that lasts the fight (uses a consumable slot); a lantern is a pricier trinket that's always on while equipped. Gear doesn't affect magical darkness.
  - Shadow Cloak works in natural night too, so night fights are the Night cleric's time to shine (kept on purpose, Oct 26, 2026).
- **Area effects** (decided Oct 23, 2026): area spells and attacks hit everyone inside, allies included, unless the card says it only affects foes. Divine magic picks its targets: all cleric and god spell areas affect foes only (e.g. Dawn Pulse, Pillar of Light, Spirit Hammer, Earthquake). In general area spells make targets save (half damage on a pass) rather than flipping to hit; each spell's method is being set in a pass.
- **Delayed spells** (decided Oct 23, 2026): a spell that lands later (e.g. Meteor) still resolves if its caster is downed first; it isn't concentration.
- **Fleeing** (decided Oct 23, 2026): a fleeing foe moves away for a turn and can't attack, then returns to the fight.
- **Hidden** (decided Oct 22, 2026; "invisible" in card text means hidden): a hidden character can't be targeted by foes, and foes are unaware of them. Hidden ends when the character attacks or takes another hostile action; moving is fine.
- Concentration: a caster can sustain only one ongoing effect at a time (a summon, a buff or a zone). Curses don't use concentration (decided Oct 26, 2026): each lasts the time its card states, and if it doesn't say, until the end of the cursed foe's next turn, so several foes can be cursed at once. Starting a new one ends the old one.
- When a target passes its save against a damage spell, it takes half damage and ignores any rider effect (root, blind, slow, etc.).
- Design principle: 3 AP cards (e.g. Megaton Punch, Knockout, Aimed Shot) take a whole turn, which is what gives AP support (Action Surge and future ally moves that grant AP) a strong payoff: extra AP becomes another big hit. Guidelines: big cards cost the full 3 AP so two in one turn needs support; extra AP stays rare and visible (a cooldown, concentration or once-per-fight card); self discounts like Wind-Up and Flow are fine as long as support payoffs stay bigger.
- Terrain effects on the grid: see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Exploration and terrain.

## Class action cards

- The number of class action cards at level 1 depends on the class. Martials (Fighter, Rogue, Ranger) gain one more card at level 3; casters pick 2 spells at level 1 and 2 at level 3 (decided Oct 21, 2026; see each spell section). All classes then gain cards from their subclass. Level 5: martials get 1 new subclass card plus 1 upgrade to a class card; casters get 1 spell pick. Level 10: no new card from the level-up; the reward is the subclass quest's subclass-specific gear (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Subclass quests). Casters' level-10 Library-style visit gives a spell pick (see each caster's spell section). Level 15: 1 new card (martials 1 new subclass card, casters 1 spell pick). Later levels: progression pass. (Decided Oct 22, 2026.)
- **Where cards come from vs where they improve** (decided Oct 22, 2026): new cards and spells come only from level-ups. Class locations (Training Grounds, Library, Meditation Rock, Tree of Life, College, Thieves Guild) only upgrade your subclass cards and teach skills; they don't upgrade class cards, since subclasses already do that at level 5. Exceptions: the Holy Defender's later god spell (meditations at Meditation Rock grow the god bond; see Half-classes), weapon mastery nodes at Training Grounds (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations), and story companions' personal quests, which can grant that companion new action cards (decided Oct 27, 2026; see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Side quests). Gear can also carry its own cards.
- Design intent: martials and casters are built differently. Martials (Fighter, Rogue, Ranger) care about weapon arts and techniques, and balance personal skills (e.g. Second Wind) against weapon techniques. Casters (Wizard, Cleric, Bard) really only need to cast spells.
- Martials keep fixed class cards at levels 1 and 3 (e.g. Second Wind, Action Surge, Sneak Attack, Poison Blade), so subclass upgrades always have a card to upgrade. Casters get more class cards (2 picks at levels 1 and 3); martials make up the gap through weapon cards (see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md)), and concentration plus half damage on saves limits caster stacking. Summoners get no exception to concentration: one summon at a time. Caster area spells stay at level 3, since martials already get area attacks from certain weapons.
- Level 15 cards and later-level cards: being filled in (Oct 22, 2026 class pass).

## Wizard spells

Wizard spells are organized into four schools matching the subclasses: Summoner, Blaster, Deceiver and Ritualist. School sizes (level 1 / level 3, decided Oct 22, 2026): Summoner, Deceiver and Ritualist have 2 / 2, Blaster 3 / 3. Every school then has 6 later spells in two tiers of 3 (see Wizard: Library spell picks). Wizards choose two spells at level 1 and two at level 3, so they can branch out. Before level 5, wizards can freely pick spells from any school to explore; players will naturally lean toward the school that fits their play style, then specialize when they choose a subclass at level 5. A wizard's INT makes enemy saves against their spells harder.

**Summoner school:** summons are creatures called up from the souls in spell materials. Summons act on their own. To balance action economy, summon spells cost more AP than other spells, and summons themselves get only 2 AP per turn (e.g. one move and one attack). Summons count as sustained spells under the concentration rule, so a caster keeps only one summon (or other ongoing effect) at a time. Summons last until destroyed or until combat ends. Summons are arcane rather than nature-themed (bound spirits and animated objects), keeping animal summons free for a possible future druid. Spells: level 1: Animated Blade (a floating sword that fights for you) and Wisp (a small bound soul that flies and zaps enemies at range). Level 3: Stone Sentinel (a sturdy construct that taunts: nearby foes must attack it) and Soul Swap (trade places with any of your summons). Later (tier and pick rules: see Library spell picks): Bound Horror (a powerful creature bound from a monster's soul, which might break free if the caster loses focus: each round the wizard makes an INT save to keep control of it, needing more heads each round it stays out; on a failure it becomes a third party that can attack allies or enemies. A freed Horror is no longer the wizard's concentration: it can't be ended by starting another concentration effect or by Unbind, and must be fought until destroyed or the fight ends. It acts in a third phase each round, after enemies, attacking the nearest creature. Decided Oct 22, 2026) and Unbind (release your current summon in a burst of soul energy that damages foes around it; ends the summon. Summoning and unbinding in the same turn is intended play. It can't be used on a Bound Horror that has broken free. Decided Oct 22, 2026).

**Blaster school:** direct elemental damage. How each spell resolves: see Wizard spell resolution. Elements for now: fire, ice and lightning. Each has its own identity: fire is big damage and big area effects, ice slows, and lightning jumps between targets. Spells: level 1: Firebolt (one heavy-hitting shot), Frost Ray (damage plus slow) and Shocking Grasp (a melee jolt that pushes the target back). Level 3: Flame Burst (a small explosion hitting a few hexes around the target), Chain Spark (lightning that jumps to a second target) and Ice Wall (a short wall of ice across a few hexes that blocks movement and line of sight; it can be broken). Later (see Library spell picks): Fireball (big area fire damage), Blizzard (an ice zone that slows everything inside and deals cold damage each turn; concentration) and Chain Lightning (a bolt that jumps from foe to foe, weakening with each jump; each jump reaches a foe within 3 hexes of the last, up to 4 foes in all; decided Oct 23, 2026). Later levels: Storm Call (3 AP, concentration: a storm cloud strikes a random enemy each turn), plus Meteor and Glacier (see Library spell picks).

**Ritualist school:** cultist-flavored magic built on barriers and blood arts. Blood arts cost HP on top of AP, letting the Ritualist trade health for extra power. Spells: level 1: Blood Bolt (pay some HP for a strong hit) and Warding Circle (a circle on the ground, one hex plus its 6 neighbours; allies standing in it take less damage; concentration. Decided Oct 22, 2026). Level 3: Blood Ward (pay HP to give an ally a damage-absorbing barrier; a new Blood Ward on the same ally replaces the old one) and Hemorrhage (curse an enemy so it bleeds each turn; CON save to resist). Later (see Library spell picks): Crimson Pact (3 AP plus a big chunk of HP: every ally, summons and beasts included, gains 1 bonus AP this turn; decided Oct 22, 2026) and Soul Siphon (drain HP from a foe to heal yourself, refilling HP for more blood arts; CON save).

**Deceiver school:** wastes enemy turns rather than dealing heavy damage. Core ideas are decoys and turning enemies on each other. Decoys pop after one hit; they last longer at higher levels (exact rules TBD). Spells: level 1: Mirror Image (copies of you appear; enemies may hit a copy instead) and Phantom Ally (a fake party member that enemies chase and attack). Level 3: Mirror Burst (pop one of your decoys on purpose; foes adjacent to it are blinded, INT save; replaced Swap, Oct 22, 2026) and Whisper (convince one enemy its ally is the real threat, so its next attack hits that ally; changed from a full turn, Oct 23, 2026, to stay apart from the Con Artist's Double-Cross). Later (see Library spell picks): Puppet Strings (on a failed CHA save, you choose the foe's actions on its next turn, spending its own AP; decided Oct 22, 2026) and Now You See Me (renamed from Vanish, Oct 22, 2026: you become hidden and leave a decoy where you stood; decoy rules as above. "Invisible" means hidden; hiding and striking with Psychic Spike each turn is allowed. Decided Oct 22, 2026). Saves: Mirror Image and Phantom Ally, INT (seeing through the illusion); Mirror Burst, INT; Whisper, WIS (resisting doubt); Puppet Strings, CHA (force of will); Now You See Me, no save.

## Cleric spells

- Clerics pick one of the eight gods at character creation; Holy Defenders pick theirs at level 5, when they take the subclass. (The gods: [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Pantheon.)
- Cleric orders (Light, Night, Good, Evil) cut across gods: any god can have clerics of any order.
- The four orders act as spell schools, like the wizard's. Each cleric's chosen god also adds spells of its own.
- Clerics pick 2 spells at level 1 and 2 at level 3. After that they follow the wizard's pattern (decided Oct 25, 2026): 1 pick each at level 5 and 15 (level-ups) and at the level-10 and level-20 Meditation Rock visits, from their order's later spells. Tier 1 opens at level 5, tier 2 at level 15, and tier 1 stays open. Each visit also teaches a skill (Lore, Persuade, Perception or Survival).
- Before level 5, clerics can pick from any order; they specialize when they choose their order (subclass) at level 5.
- **Order identities** (decided Oct 23, 2026; each order's spell list is being rebuilt to match):
  - **Light, radiant battle-priest:** mid-range radiant magic that burns and blinds foes near allies, protecting the front line.
  - **Night, shadow and fear** (replaces Order of Dark): darkness zones, frightening foes and hiding allies in shadow; control through fear.
  - **Good, miracles:** rare, big effects (cleansing every status, preventing a downing, once-per-fight saves) rather than steady healing, which keeps it apart from the Musician bard.
  - **Evil, draining curses:** the inverse of Good. Curses that drain: cursed foes lose HP that flows to the cleric or allies.
  - Necromancy (raising skeletons, ghosts and the like) is dropped from clerics for now; summoning is the wizard Summoner's.
- Splits from the wizard schools: Evil drains life through its god's favour, while the wizard Ritualist spends its own blood and sets up barriers.
- A cleric's WIS makes enemy saves against their spells harder.

## Bard spells

- Bard spells are performances; each subclass is a school built on its art (Narrator: storytelling, Musician: music, Dancer: dance, Leader: command).
- Bards pick 2 spells at level 1, 2 at level 3 and 1 per unlock after that, from any school until they choose their subclass at level 5.
- A bard's CHA makes enemy saves against their spells harder.
- Leader bards have a weapon focus, unlike the other casters.

## Instruments

Decided Oct 25, 2026 (rebuilt Oct 26 from the summary in Cards & progression after the original section was lost in a save).

- Anyone can learn an instrument at the College for gold, or swap the one they know (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations).
