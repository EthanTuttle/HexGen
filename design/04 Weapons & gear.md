# HexGen — Weapons & gear

Last updated Oct 30, 2026 · @Ethan · Part of the [Index](00 Index.md)

Equipment, weapon and armor categories, weapon rules, weapon category cards, weapon mastery trees, loot, inventory and gold.

## Equipment

- Equipment slots: 2 swappable weapon sets, armor, accessories/trinkets, 2 consumables. Only equipped gear is slot-limited; spare gear and consumables go in the inventory (see Loot, inventory and gold → Inventory).
- Gear has three rarity tiers: Common, Rare, Legendary. (Map cards no longer use rarity: see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Card types.)
- Equipped gear shows on the character's mini (everything except amulets and rings): see [Presentation](07 Presentation.md) → Art direction.
- **Accessory and trinket slots:** helmet, leggings, boots, gloves, 2 rings, 1 amulet. Some helmets, leggings, boots and gloves carry an armor category (light/medium/heavy) and follow the class armor limits; others have no category and anyone can wear them.
- **Consumables:** basic consumables such as potions and throwable items. No limit on how many can be carried in the inventory, but each character can equip only 2 consumables for use in combat.

## Weapon and armor categories

**Weapon categories:** greatswords, longswords, shortswords, rapiers, scimitars, daggers, battleaxes, greataxes, maces, warhammers, spears, halberds, glaives, staves, wands, shortbows, longbows, crossbows, and fist weapons: gauntlets (more defensive), brass knuckles (higher damage) and wraps (quicker, monk-like). There is no separate throwing-weapon category; weapons that can be thrown cover it.

**Armor categories:** light armor, medium armor, heavy armor, shields.

**Class gear** (Humans ignore these limits; see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Races)

| Class | Weapons | Armor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fighter | All | All, including shields |
| Rogue | Daggers, shortswords, rapiers, scimitars, shortbows, crossbows, fist weapons | Light |
| Wizard | Daggers, staves, wands | None |
| Cleric | Maces, warhammers, staves | Light, medium, shields |
| Ranger | Longswords, shortswords, scimitars, daggers, spears, glaives, shortbows, longbows, crossbows, fist weapons | Light, medium |
| Bard | Rapiers, longswords, shortswords, daggers, shortbows, crossbows | Light |

Leader bards also gain medium armor and polearms (see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Bard spells).

## Starting gear

Each class chooses from the most basic weapons and armor in its own categories. The player picks each piece in the character creator: main hand, offhand and armour, one by one (decided Oct 30, 2026). **One weapon set** (decided Oct 30, 2026): a new hero picks a single set; the second set starts empty and fills from loot. **Humans** (Versatile) can pick from every basic weapon and armour, not just their class's (decided Oct 30, 2026; the trait: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Races). Background starter items: see [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Backgrounds.

## Weapon rules

- Design intent: weapons carry their own action cards. This is what makes weapon combat as interesting and varied as spell combat. To keep martials as interesting as casters, the loot pool needs lots of weapons with interesting effects or special cards; this is the main way martials grow their options, where casters grow through spell picks. Martials get 1–2 cards per weapon, both sets when dual-wielding, and two swappable weapon sets.
- Weapon action cards come by weapon category (shared by all weapons of that category); the number varies by category. Rarity doesn't change category cards: higher rarity adds stats, and special weapons add extra cards of their own.
- Each category sets the ability its attacks use: STR for heavy weapons, DEX for finesse and ranged weapons, and INT or WIS for wands and staves.
- **Hands:** two-handed weapons (greatsword, greataxe, warhammer, halberd, glaive, shortbow, longbow, crossbow) leave no hand free for a shield or an offhand weapon. Versatile weapons (longsword, battleaxe, spear, staff) can be used one- or two-handed; two-handed only when nothing is in the other hand, and deal more damage when held two-handed. One-handed weapons: shortsword, rapier, scimitar, dagger, mace, wand.
- **Weapon sets:** a weapon set is a main hand plus an offhand (a weapon or a shield), or a single two-handed weapon. **Swapping sets mid-fight** (decided Oct 25, 2026) costs 0 AP, once per turn; the hotbar swaps with it (see [Presentation](07 Presentation.md) → Action cards screen).
- **Weapon modifiers** are always on and aren't cards, so they cost no AP (Long Range, Point Blank and Backstab moved from cards to modifiers, Oct 25, 2026):
  - **Reach** (spear, halberd, glaive): all attacks, including the basic attack, can hit 2 hexes away.
  - **Long Range** (longbow): shoots farther than normal.
  - **Point Blank** (crossbow): no ranged penalty when an enemy is adjacent (see Ranged).
  - **Backstab** (dagger): the first dagger hit from behind each turn deals bonus damage. It stacks with the Rogue's Sneak Attack.
- **Ranged:** ranged attacks have a higher coin target when an enemy is adjacent; the crossbow's Point Blank modifier removes that penalty.
- **Wand basic attack** (decided Oct 25, 2026): a wand's basic attack is Magic Bolt, a basic ranged spell (1 AP, repeatable, like any basic attack). Wands have no category cards.
- **Thrown weapons** (any weapon that can be thrown, e.g. daggers, spears and battleaxes) return to the thrower's hand automatically.
- **Dual-wielding:** any class can dual-wield one-handed weapons. Once per turn, after a main-hand attack, the offhand weapon makes a free attack at full strength. A dual-wielder gets both weapons' category cards.
- **Fist weapons** are three separate categories, each with its own category cards and mastery tree. They are worn as a pair, taking both hands and counting as dual-wielding (the free offhand hit once per turn), so no shield. All martials can use them and learn their mastery. Gauntlets and brass knuckles use STR; wraps use DEX.

## Weapon mastery

Training at Training Grounds (a class location: see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Class locations) is tied to a weapon category, not a fixed list, since Leaders and Fighters can use many weapons. Each training picks one weapon category and both grants an extra mastery card for that category (on top of its normal category cards) and upgrades that category's existing cards (e.g. Reach gains a hex, Cleave hits harder). Switching weapons means training a new category.

- Leader bards: one training per visit, gated at levels 10, 15 and 20 (3 total).
- Fighters, Rogues and Rangers use the same system. By level 15, Fighters get 6 trainings and Rogues and Rangers 3 (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Levels and milestones; which levels they unlock at: progression pass).

### Mastery tree framework

- Mastery is learned per weapon category and works with any weapon of that category.
- Each category has a skill tree with 2 branches (e.g. single-target vs crowd) of 3 nodes each, the third being a capstone: 6 nodes per tree.
- Each training unlocks one node. Nodes within a branch unlock in order. Only Fighters can mix nodes from different branches of the same weapon; other martials commit to one branch per weapon. This is the Fighter's niche: the true weapon master.
- Each node is a new card, an upgrade to a card, or a passive (e.g. Heavy Blade, Unshakable). The mix of node types varies by weapon.
- **Branch names** (decided Oct 27, 2026): a branch is named for the wielder who fights that way (Duelist, Thrower, Warlord), not for a thing or a style of attack. Fist-weapon branches (gauntlets, wraps) use real fighting styles (Wrestler, Boxer, Karateka, Taekwondoin). Kept as they were: Shadow and Volley. Renamed Oct 27: Battlefield → Warlord, Longblade → Blademaster, Phalanx → Legionary, Pike → Lancer, Rapid Fire → Rapid Shot, Flurry → Striker, Bloodaxe → Ravager, Hooker → Harrier, Grappler → Wrestler, Counter → Boxer, Flow → Karateka, Wind Step → Taekwondoin, Heavy Bolts → Arbalist, Bulwark → Guardian, Bash → Basher.
- **Weapon passives on the tree** (decided Oct 27, 2026): a weapon's always-on modifiers (Reach, Long Range, Point Blank, Backstab: see Weapon rules) show on the mastery tree as passives beside its category cards, not as part of a card.
- **Node names** (decided Oct 26, 2026): every node has a name on the tree. A new-card node uses the card's name. An upgrade node renames the card it changes, by the upgrade-name rule ([Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Martial class cards): more of the same adds a "+", a new effect gives a new name. Only a card's first upgrade can rename it; every later upgrade adds a "+" (Aimed Shot → Piercing Shot → Piercing Shot+ → Piercing Shot++; Throw → Throw+ → Throw++). Upgrades to an always-on modifier follow the same rule (Backstab+, Reach+). Passive nodes with no card get a short name of their own (e.g. Unshakable, Bloodletter).
- **Mixed branches** (Fighters only): when a Fighter takes the first upgrade from two branches that change the same card, the card gets a mix name: gauntlets Guard Up → Perfect Preparation, wraps Step and Strike → Flowing Wind. Brass knuckles stays Haymaker++, since both of its upgrades are more of the same.
- Each weapon card (category and mastery cards alike) can be used once per turn. This is what balances cheap weapon skills: e.g. a Fighter can Cleave once, then make two basic attacks.
- Branches are about the weapon itself, not its grip. Versatile weapons (longsword, battleaxe, spear, staff) get a third branch (3 nodes) focused on the two-handed style; its cards and upgrades only work while the weapon is held two-handed. The number of trainings is unchanged, so this adds choice, not power.
- Who gets mastery: all martials (Fighter, Rogue, Ranger) plus the Leader bard as a half-class. Casters don't; staves and wands have no mastery trees and stay simple, since casters grow through spells.
- Where martials train: all martials (and the Leader) train weapon mastery at Training Grounds.

## Loot, inventory and gold

- Loot (items and gear) is separate from map cards. The loot pool holds gear and items (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Deck and hand).
- **Inventory:** the party shares one inventory (not one per character). It has no cap on spare gear or consumables; only equipped gear uses slots. Multiplayer: see [Multiplayer & tech](06 Multiplayer & tech.md) → Multiplayer.
- **Gold sources:** fights (scaling with difficulty), quest rewards, selling spare loot in towns, and finished village regions (e.g. market towns paying out gold or trade). Gold pays for inns, camp kits and shops.
