## Art direction

**Reference 2: Dorfromantik** (the primary art direction: the game shifts to this style over Reference 1)

- Placed hexes blend at their edges into continuous regions: forests merge into woods, fields into patchwork farmland, rivers into lakes, houses into towns. Hex outlines are only faintly visible on the grass.
- Unplaced space is a flat, soft pastel backdrop (pink in this image). In Dorfromantik the pastel colour changes over time; Heads or Hexes keeps a pastel backdrop that changes colour by its own rules (chosen Oct 21, 2026):
  - **Time of day (main rule):** the pastel drifts with the day clock, for example soft peach at dawn, pale sky-blue at midday and rose at dusk, with a darker, cooler pastel at night. It doubles as a quiet hint of how close nightfall is. Combat pauses the clock, so a fight keeps the colour it started with. (Day clock: see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Day and night.)
    - **The light** (chosen Oct 30, 2026, from the Godot prototype; revisit in playtesting): it colours the land as well as the backdrop. Warm light and long shadows at dawn and dusk; at night it's cooler and dimmer but still readable, with lanterns and glowing toadstools standing out. The values blend smoothly between these hours (Godot: `scripts/time_of_day.gd` and the loop's backdrops in `scripts/game/game.gd`):

      | Time (hour) | Backdrop | Sun colour | Sun strength | Sun height | Ambient light |
      | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
      | Night (20:00–4:30) | #A9AECB | #AFC0E8 (moonlight) | 0.35 | 35° | 0.5 |
      | Dawn (6:30) | #F4DCCB | #FFE2C8 | 1.1 | 24°, rising in the east | 0.7 |
      | Morning and afternoon (9:00, 15:00) | #D9E7EC | #FFFFFF | 1.18 | 30° | 0.55 |
      | Midday (12:00) | #D9E7EC | #FFFFFF | 1.18 | 38° | 0.5 |
      | Dusk (18:00) | #EDCFD7 | #FFD8CC | 1.1 | 24°, setting in the west | 0.7 |

      Ambient light is lifted when the sun is low so dense shade (the deep forest) doesn't go black. The water dims at night. The Blender previews use the midday light with the older backdrop #CFE4F2.
  - **Story progress (slow layer):** as the rift grows each chapter, the pastel shifts gradually toward an uneasy lilac on top of the time-of-day colour. (The rift: see [World & story](01 World & story.md).)
  - Not used: tinting by the terrain underfoot (risks the contrast problem of combat v9b–v9d), and a fixed colour per campaign.
- Along the map's edge, empty slots show as pale, translucent hexes; gaps inside the map show the same way.
- Stylized, low-poly look with a warm palette; tilted top-down camera.
- **Scale and hex orientation** (chosen Oct 29, 2026): minis are at **true scale** everywhere. A mini's base fills one combat cell, and a map tile is the size of the combat board it grows into (radius 4, 61 cells), so on the map a mini's base is about an eighth of the tile's width. **Map tiles are pointy-top** (as in the mockups) and **combat cells are flat-top**, so the board's outline is pointy-top and matches the tile it grew from. Combat v14 still draws pointy-top cells; the next version takes this. Prototype units: [Multiplayer & tech](06 Multiplayer & tech.md) → Engine prototype.
- Small life on the map: chimney smoke, boats on the water, deer in the forest.
- **Water flows freely** (chosen Oct 29, 2026): water never follows the hex grid's straight lines.
  - **Rivers** are smooth, meandering curves that cross each tile edge at its midpoint, so they flow on from tile to tile as one river.
  - **Lakes:** a water tile becomes lake when it and two of its water neighbours all touch each other (three tiles around a corner). All such tiles form one lake with gently wobbly shores, and rivers flow into and out of it. A line of water tiles with no such triangle stays a river.
  - **Ponds:** a water tile with no water neighbours is a small round pond.
  - **Stable while building:** a tile's water shape depends only on its own water neighbours, so placing a tile reshapes only that tile and the ones touching it.
  - **Look:** a slow ripple and sparkle that flows along rivers, a light foam rim at the shore and a darker deep middle, bank dressing (reeds, rocks, lily pads, a small jetty), and the boats above on lakes and wide rivers.
- **Town scenes** (chosen Nov 4, 2026): the town square and inn stand on one floor with hexes set into it by dark joints, focused and real rather than separate plates. In the inn, the innkeeper stands behind the bar, on a dark warm backdrop.
- **Starting tiles** (chosen Nov 4, 2026): each background's place is a world tile on its terrain, with its door facing the map camera.

- **Buried city** (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026; in development): a city of turquoise domes and slim towers, half-drifted and broken, under sand whose level rises and falls by tile as storms pass, from only spire tips showing to whole streets. Where the sand drops, a few pits open under broken domes, with sand pouring into dark chambers; the biggest, a square hall with a stair down to a tall door and four corner pillars that still show when it's buried, holds the ancient library. Storms are rolling, bubbling clouds of low-poly spheres in varying colours and opacity, hiding tiles as their levels change (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026; the flat sheets were tried and dropped). Who built it isn't decided.
- **Feywild crossing** (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026; in development): each tile is locked in one of the four seasons, with hex edges as the sharp seams between them and one forest carrying across, so a tree on a seam splits into both seasons and trails and logs run through. Winter has snowy pines and bare-limbed broadleaves. Lighting isn't locked: the ordinary day and night cycle lights every tile. Outer edges stay sharp, an exception to variants blending into the land around them, though tree crowns may overhang them. Trails only run on routes toward the Fey Gate, with forest filling the rest. **Fey Gate:** two great trees grown into a pointed arch, with four round crowns, one per season, coming off the two intertwined branches at the top, so it reads as intertwined trees, around a mystical light-purple portal, with wisps of light drifting into it.
- **Ruins land** (rethought from the ground up, Ethan, Nov 8, 2026; replaces the Nov 4 hex-column rule): ruins are the remains of vague, long-lost civilizations (lore: [World & story](01 World & story.md) → Ruins, Nov 9, 2026). Ruins tiles read by **stone ground**: mostly paved or rubble, little grass, which sets them apart from plains and desert. On it stand **mixed remains** (Ethan, Nov 9, 2026): a jumble of walls, arches, columns and statues from no one style, as vague as the lore. **Rebuilt as structure** (Ethan, Nov 9, 2026, after comparing the ruins with the forest, town and farm regions; reopens the approved plain tiles and Overrun outpost): like those regions, ruins become one mass filling the tile, made of connected remains (roofless rooms of low broken walls, rubble mounds, and overgrown building footprints, mixed tile to tile) laid along clear streets that carry across edges, with heights in bands rather than lone sticks. Each variant recolours that mass, not just adds props. The Warren and Crypt landmarks keep their looks. The look of Dunfaire Tower on this land is decided later.
- **Region variant looks** (chosen Oct 30, 2026): when a region finishes, its tiles take on the variant's look (no rim: see the Exploration screen's cluster goals). A variant can change whatever makes it look best (colours, props, ground shape, borders), but its tiles keep blending into the land around them, except raised hills regions, which stand on cliffs (chosen Nov 1, 2026; [Multiplayer & tech](06 Multiplayer & tech.md) → Tile build). - **Characters are tabletop-style minis** (chosen Oct 21, 2026): heroes, companions and enemies are 3D models styled like painted miniatures standing on bases, not fully animated characters. They have no walking or swinging animations; actions are shown by moving the whole mini. Attacks: the attacker does a little hop, and a mini that gets hit recoils. **Attack motions by kind** (Ethan, Nov 7, 2026): the hop suits basic melee, but other actions (sweeps, thrusts, shots, bolts like Firebolt, area spells, buffs, summons) each get their own whole-mini move plus effects (projectiles, slash arcs, sparks, impact puffs). Still no limb animation. Picked Nov 7, 2026 (Ethan, from Combat Art's previews; colours, sizes and timings are placeholders): basic melee keeps the hop; sweep, a full spin with a trail, foes hit in order; thrust, a pivot jab (tips forward on its base); heavy blow, a big hop slam with a shock ring; bow shot, a flat fast arrow (the archer rocks back); thrown, a snap throw spinning end over end; bolt spells each get their own after a short charge (Firebolt an orb, Frost Ray a held ray, Magic Bolt three curving darts); touch spells, a charge then a tap; burst spells, an orb lobbed to the centre, then a dome over the lit cells; cones (Breath Weapon), rolling puffs, each hex lighting as the first puffs reach it; buff or heal, a gold ring rising up the ally; summons drop in from above; a hit mini tips back; a miss is a sidestep. Each move stays under about 0.6 s. Cards map to these by kind (Ethan, Nov 8, 2026, placeholder): Scorch plays the Firebolt orb; Blood Bolt, wand and Wisp hits the darts; Haymaker, Bonebreaker and greatsword, greataxe and warhammer attacks the heavy slam; spear, halberd and glaive attacks the jab; foes hop when adjacent and jab from 2 away. A multi-foe sweep (Sweeping Arc) flips for every foe first, then plays one spin, each foe reacting as the trail passes it. Single-target save spells (Sacred Flame, Faerie Fire, Lull; picked Nov 8, 2026) play the bolt charge, then a ring closes round the foe's feet and the effect blooms on it; a passed save shows a weaker version (Sacred Flame's flame smaller for half damage, Faerie Fire's outline breaking into sparks, Lull's motes scattering). Lull on a fail: z's rise once and the foe gets its sleep chip; the mini doesn't lean or droop (it clipped into the ground), and waking just removes the chip (Ethan, Nov 8, 2026). **Minis show equipped gear** (chosen Oct 21, 2026): every equipped item except amulets and rings appears on the mini: weapons, shield, armour, helmet, leggings, boots and gloves. Swapping a sword for a glaive changes the mini, and enchantments show too (e.g. a fire-enchanted glaive looks on fire). This makes minis modular: gear pieces are separate parts on the figure rather than sculpted in. Only the active weapon set shows (the inactive set isn't on the mini), and equipped belt consumables don't show. **Poses follow the weapon type** held, not the character (a raised one-handed pose can't hold a glaive or a bow): one pose per weapon category (chosen Oct 21, 2026; categories: [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Weapon and armor categories), so a rapier gets a fencer's stance and a halberd a braced one. How poses are built (chosen Oct 21, 2026): each pose is authored once on a simple posing skeleton (used only to pose minis, never to animate them) and transferred to every body type, so the work scales with weapon categories rather than categories × body types; this is part of why a real 3D pass is needed later. The main-hand weapon's category sets the pose, and the offhand is handled by two arm variants (shield, second weapon) rather than a pose per combination; an empty offhand keeps the base pose's free hand (chosen Oct 21, 2026). The full list is in **Pose library** below. Versatile weapons (longsword, battleaxe, spear, staff) also get a two-handed pose. Fist weapons pose like two-handed weapons (both hands, no offhand variant); combat still treats them as dual-wielding ([Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Weapon rules).

  - **Combat maps** (chosen Nov 2, 2026; changed the same day): a fight is an animated zoom into the hex, but it really happens on a separate map outside that hex, not on the tile itself. Combat maps are custom built to look interesting and give their own tactical challenges, themed to the terrain and variant, using ground cells and blocking or cover cells (trees, rocks, fences, walls) and variant props (webs, graves, stalls). Forest is the first set. **Props and terrain shape the fight, not the board's outline** (Ethan, Nov 2, 2026): a map's cell base stays a simple, solid shape, never punched into thin strips or holes; narrow lanes and walls come from obstacles and terrain standing on cells. Maps vary in overall size for more dynamic scenes, but none is thinner than the Gorge map, and that thin only with a reason in the scene, such as a cliff wall along it (Nov 2, 2026), and obstacles can be big: full-sized trees, fallen trunks, big rocks and other objects spanning several cells, not only one rock or bush per cell (Ethan, Nov 2, 2026). Tall obstacles such as map-height trees are fine, since the camera rotates, as long as every cell can be seen from at least one angle of the rotation (chosen Nov 2, 2026). **Path maps** (chosen Nov 2, 2026): terrains with drawn paths also get themed, hand-built maps round their roads and structures (farm tracks and gates, town streets and squares, trails, bridges). A fight on a tile with paths rolls from both its path maps and its terrain maps. They're themed, not copied from the real tile, so a road's direction needn't match. The landmark structures themselves (the nomad camp's ring, the ruined war camp, spire and nest, and so on) stay off ordinary maps, saved for landmark fights, while generic props that also appear near landmarks are fine (palisades, graves, outlying tents, big trees, webs; Nov 2, 2026); herds and similar are plain obstacles (chosen Nov 2, 2026). A variant's maps differ clearly in overall shape and size, not just props (Nov 2, 2026). The list of maps in use, with a reference image of each: `art/combat/maps/README.md`. **Map count** (chosen Nov 2, 2026): 3 terrain maps per variant, plus 1 path map if the variant has roads. **High ground** (Ethan, Nov 2, 2026): rock can be walkable raised ground, thick and clearly stepped, reached by a stepped way up: a flat landing at half height, with three even steps running out into every neighbouring cell, down to low ground and up into a notch in raised ground (chosen Nov 3, 2026; replaces stone steps and log ramps). Treads count as ground, and some clipping of a neighbour's base is accepted. Alongside this, tall cliffs act as blocking walls (rules: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md)). How many maps, and how a fight picks one: open. **Maps needn't match tiles prop for prop** (Ethan, Nov 3, 2026, taking back that day's rule that they must): fight props the tiles don't have, like trunks, boulders and stumps, are fine and don't get added to tiles. **Paths on maps** (chosen Nov 3, 2026): earth paths and furrows use path-coloured cells, as on farmland, not a texture laid over the cells. **Houses on maps** (chosen Nov 3, 2026): each stands on stone covering exactly the cells it blocks, on 3 or 4 cells in a mix, with household things on the leftover stone so it doesn't look empty: true to a mini's scale, with colour and clear shapes, in small groups on every side, so a house doesn't look plain when the camera turns, with the default camera's side clearest (props chosen Nov 3, 2026: woodpile, water, garden and yard-animal sets, ladders, lanterns, net racks and flower boxes on houses, with variant-specific yards as each set comes up). A house's front and back don't mirror each other, and not every house has a back door. Nothing stands in front of a door, and house yards stay inside their cells. Other props may overhang their cell by up to 0.15 (a cell is 1.0 across; chosen Nov 3, 2026). Existing farm buildings over that are exempt until their own pass; some roofs vary so they don't read flat from the default camera. **Slum shacks** (chosen Nov 3, 2026): small cores with crooked lean-tos and a stacked room, under patched roofs, rather than whole buildings leaning. **Washing lines** (chosen Nov 3, 2026) only stand next to a house group. **Scholars' Town maps** (Ethan, Nov 3, 2026) are built around nice courtyards and walkways, not buildings scattered on open lawn. **Cliff lifts** (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026): tall cliffs are climbed through a one-cell-wide way of hex cells rising 0.45 each, with long steps on each rise like the ways up. **Volcanic maps** (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026): peaks are the tile's separate smooth cones with lit craters, in place of the Hills peak clusters. It moves as hard flat bands: two or three flat colours with crisp edges drifting downstream in the surface itself, with nothing floating on top (Nov 4, 2026). Crater pools, cracks and streaks match its vibrant colours, in fights and on volcanic map tiles (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026); on the cone it runs in a carved channel between raised dark banks, and it hangs off the board's edge as a heavy, uneven mass (Nov 4, 2026). **Fight camera** (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026): a fight starts close on the party and first foes with minis big and readable, and players pan to see the rest, rather than fitting the whole map. Plain plains maps are big and spread out, to feel like wide open space. **Hills peaks** (Ethan, Nov 3, 2026): hills maps use the tiles' big clusters of sharp peaks, one peak to a blocked cell and far taller than a mini, not lone mountains or solid ridges. Plain Hills maps also take darker pines and more trees; other variants keep the trees their looks give them. Highlands maps each carry a few clan banners, kept limited (Ethan, Nov 4, 2026). **Hills slopes** (chosen Nov 3, 2026): hills maps slope as a whole, level cells on an uneven hill's shape. ## UI

**UI style: "Game pieces"** (chosen Oct 21, 2026, from three mockup directions)

- Hex shape language, as in Dorfromantik's menus: buttons, gear slots, portraits and inventory items are hexes. Pill buttons and tabs have pointed hex ends.
- UI elements look like painted wooden board-game tokens, with a visible solid edge underneath that gives them thickness.
- Light, cream-toned menu pieces sit on a soft, muted backdrop, so the menu contrasts clearly with the background.
- **Backdrops:** true menus (such as the character and gear screen) keep the plain, muted menu backdrop. Screens that show the world (such as combat) use the world's soft pastel backdrop instead (chosen: combat v9a). Rejected for combat: terrain-themed backdrops (v9b–v9d), which lose contrast against the battlefield tiles; the ring of pale empty hexes (v9e), since empty slots only matter when placing tiles and nothing goes in them during combat.
- Rejected directions: "Airy white" (flat white hexes on pale sky; too much blue, and closest to Dorfromantik) and "Cartographer's map" (ink and parchment; too much beige).

**Item display**

- Gear slots and inventory items show only an icon, with no text label. This leaves room for bigger hexes.
- Item details appear in a pop-up: on hover on PC, on long-press on mobile.
- Rarity shows as the hex's rim colour (Common, Rare, Legendary: see [Weapons & gear](04 Weapons & gear.md) → Equipment).

**Screen text** (rules chosen Oct 26, 2026; every mockup follows them from its next version, and older artboards stay as they are)

- **Show results, not the balance behind them.** No fight difficulty labels ("easy fight"), draw amounts, or reasons a reward was bigger (e.g. night fights drawing more). Other balance details are judged case by case, keeping the ones the player weighs a choice with. Kept so far: the coin check line ("3 coins · STR · needs 1 heads"), the region countdown badge, the hand, deck and discard counts, and the numbers in a card's effect.
- **Let players find combos** (Oct 27, 2026): card, passive and item text describes only what that thing does. It never points out how it stacks or combos with other cards, classes or gear (e.g. Backstab's text doesn't mention the Rogue's Sneak Attack, though they stack); players find those themselves.
- **Card text says what happens** ("gain 3 bonus AP this turn"), with no extra maths or commentary ("net +2").
- **No hints for standard input.** Hover, long-press, tap and drag work the same everywhere and are taught once in onboarding, so no screen says "Hover or long-press for details".
- **Don't restate what a visual already shows.** If a bar, badge, icon or animation shows it, no sentence repeats it: an XP bar that fills needs no "The party reached level 5", and cards drawn need no "Into your hand".
- **Panels get a short title, then values and icons.** Anything that needs explaining goes in a pop-up or the details panel.
- **Character subtitles: "Race Class (Subclass) · Lv"** (chosen Oct 30, 2026), e.g. "Human Fighter (Tactician) · Lv 5", "Wood Elf Rogue (Con Artist) · Lv 5". No role word: "Hero" and "Companion" are design terms, not the player's, and the player may not see their character as a hero. The level-up, action cards and weapon mastery screens take this in their next versions (they show "Human Fighter · Hero", "Dragonborn Cleric · Companion · Lv …" and similar).
- **Sentences that stay:** a line that explains an icon (e.g. the details panel's "Once per fight: stays used until the next fight"), and helper prompts that stop a mistake (e.g. the End phase pop-up's "Unspent AP is lost when the phase ends").
- **Blurbs** (style set Oct 26, 2026 from Ethan's Fighter blurbs; the blurbs themselves: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Subclass blurbs): one sentence of about 10–12 words. It opens with "A" plus an adjective or role and an archetype noun ("A mighty warrior", "A watchful master of war"), then a short clause on how they fight or what drives them ("who …", "whose …" or a comma and an "-ing" verb). Flavour, not mechanics: no card names, numbers or AP, and a half-class's borrowed class is hinted at, not named. Vary the archetype nouns within a class (the Cleric drafts leaned on "priest" three times).
- Cut under these rules (in each screen's next version): fight difficulty, draw notes, the details hint and the level-up sentence on the after-combat screen (v3); its "To the shared inventory" subtitle (v4); combat's Move rule sentence; exploration's night-building toast; the level-up screen's "Milestone rewards are shared by everyone" and its doc references.

## Screen mockups

**How mockups are kept**

- **Working canvas, one per screen or system:** every iteration lives there and is never overwritten. Each change is a new numbered copy (v1, v2 … with letter variants such as v8a–v8d when comparing options), and rejected versions stay for reference.
- **Final screens canvas, one for the whole game:** it holds only approved versions, one artboard per screen, side by side. Finals are copied from a working canvas, never edited directly. A change goes through the working canvas first and is then copied over as the new final.
- **These docs stay the source of truth:** the rules for each screen are written in the UI section above. Mockups illustrate them.
- Canvases are private until shared from the page's Share menu.

**How to make a mockup** (agreed Oct 21, 2026; follow this every time)

1. **Format:** always a Design canvas (the Artifact "Design" type), never a hand-built HTML page.
2. **One working canvas per screen,** titled "Heads or Hexes · <Screen> (working)", but each screen is designed as part of the flow. Copy in the latest artboards of the screens that lead into and out of it, and link them so the buttons that open the new screen (and its back button) work in Play. If a connected screen already does part of the new screen's job, settle how they fit together before building, and update that screen's artboard too.
3. **Study before building:** read the latest artboards of related canvases and reuse their parts: top bar, panels, trays, hex tokens, card anatomy.
4. **Artboard spec:** 844×390 phone landscape, corner radius 28, interactive. Match the fonts, colours and parts of the latest artboards (the palette and fonts are still placeholders; see Open questions).
5. **Iterating:** every change is a new numbered artboard (v2, v3a…), titled "<Screen> vN · what changed", with a row heading per version. Old artboards are never edited.
6. **Same turn:** update the screen's rules in the UI section and the mockup index below, and copy the changed canvas files into `mockups/` (see [mockups/README.md](../mockups/README.md)).
7. **Finals:** approved versions are copied into the Final screens canvas, never edited there.
8. **Placeholders** (agreed Oct 26, 2026): anything undecided shows on the artboard as a bracketed value (e.g. "+[gold]", "[Hero name]"), with no explaining sentence beside it. Notes about what isn't set go in the artboard's canvas title, not on the artboard. Every placeholder gets an open question in the doc that owns the rule, and a row in Mockup placeholders below.

## Camera and controls

- Camera: free pan, zoom and rotate.
- **One set of camera controls for every hex screen** (decided Oct 28, 2026): the map, combat, and social and town scenes all pan, zoom and rotate the same way. Touch: the gestures above. Mouse: a drag pans, the scroll wheel zooms, a right-drag rotates, and the nav button beside equipment opens the zoom, rotate and frame buttons. Scenes got the camera back in social v9 and town v4 (social v4–v8 had left the nav button out). The map and combat mockups don't have the wheel or right-drag yet; they get them in their next versions.
- **The opened nav buttons overlap the cards** (Oct 28, 2026): opened to the right, the five camera buttons slide under the option cards and the shop counter (and likely the map's hand). Being compared in town v5: **v5a, a fan** (a small honeycomb above the nav button, over the open board); **v5b, a pop-up tray** (a wood tray above the nav button, on top of everything); **v5c, the cards make room** (the buttons open to the right as before and the card row slides over). In v5a and v5b a tap on the board closes them. v6a and v6b move the fan and the tray in close to the nav button (v5's sat too far from it): the fan's hexes hug it up and to the right, and the tray sits right on top of it with its tail pointing at it; both cover the cards while open. **v6c, nav between** (Oct 28, 2026): the nav button moves between Quests and equipment, the three hexes touching in a zig-zag (Quests on top, the nav button half a hex to the right, equipment below), and opened, the camera buttons fan out against it on the right as in v6a.
- **Tap, drag or hold** (Oct 28, 2026): a press that barely moves and lets go quickly is a tap: it moves a scene's line on or picks what's under it. Moving past a small distance makes it a drag (the camera pans); a long hold without moving is a long-press (details, as everywhere) and never continues a line. Mockup thresholds are placeholders (8 px, 350 ms).
- **Reframing in scenes** (chosen Oct 28, 2026): the camera stays where the player puts it during dialogue, and eases back to frame the action when the scene changes: a fight starting (the hex grows into a combat map), entering or leaving the inn, or opening the shop counter. The nav button's centre button frames the scene again.
- **Phone screen edges** (chosen Oct 30, 2026): the board and backdrop fill the whole screen, edge to edge, and show behind the Dynamic Island. The UI keeps out of the island's strip: on whichever side the island is (it swaps when the phone turns round), the trays, hand and buttons shift in by the device's safe-area inset, so the layout sits slightly right with the island on the left and slightly left with it on the right. The home bar is hidden while playing.
- **Long-press** fires as soon as a still press passes the tap time (350 ms, placeholder), with no extra wait (chosen Oct 30, 2026).
- **Long-press on the board** (chosen Oct 30, 2026): nothing on an empty slot; on a map hex, a small pop-up with its terrain, its region and progress toward the cluster goal, and the region's variant once finished. On a combat token, the character's details: an enemy's name, HP and what it does; an ally's HP, AP and status. On a map encounter within sight range, those details or its offer. Hover shows the same on PC.
- **Long-press on HUD controls** (chosen Oct 31, 2026): a **party portrait** shows that character's effects (injuries, buffs, conditions) with what each does, and nothing at all if they have none. Everything else does nothing on a long-press: map cards in the hand, the Quests and equipment buttons, the nav button and its camera buttons, combat's camera buttons and End hex, and the landmark pin. On PC, hover follows the same rule.
- **Minimum text size on phones** (chosen Oct 31, 2026): **11 pt**, Apple's smallest standard text style (Caption 2). Labels now at 9–10 pt grow to 11 pt; the smallest labels need strong contrast.
- **The player's camera wins** (chosen Oct 30, 2026): the camera follows the party while it walks and centres on whoever acts in combat, but any pan, zoom or rotate by the player stops that until the next action. The nav button's centre button puts it back.
- **Touch** (added Oct 24, 2026, exploration v3): one finger drags to pan, two fingers pinch to zoom and twist to rotate (two fingers never pan, chosen Oct 30, 2026). **Rotation is free, with no snapping** (decided Oct 30, 2026: the mockups' snap to the nearest 60° step is dropped); the nav button's rotate buttons turn the view by a fixed step (size: placeholder, for playtesting). The on-screen camera buttons stay for mouse and one-handed play, behind the nav button from exploration v7 (see Exploration screen). Not yet in the combat mockups.
