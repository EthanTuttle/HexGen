## Naming

Updated Oct 30, 2026. **Inspiration, not a rule:** names lean on **Irish, Gaelic and German** roots for a shared flavour, but no name has to be built from them. Plain English, invented names and a few classical names are all fine where they suit.

- **Loose mapping:** Irish and Gaelic sounds lean toward the countryside, folk, humans, halflings, elves and the tiefling fire bloodlines; German sounds lean toward dwarves, golems, forges, strongholds and towns. A lean, not a law.
- **Keep names sayable:** use anglicised spellings rather than native ones (Dunfaire, not Dún Faire); a new player should say a name right on the first try (no silent letters, umlauts or vowel clusters like "ao"); people's names are short (1–3 syllables).
- **Handy word parts** (optional): Irish/Gaelic dun (fort), kil (church), bally (town), glen (valley), ard (high), knock (hill), derry (oak), inver (river mouth), more (big), beg (small); German -hof (farm), -berg (mountain), -brunn (well), -wald (forest), -feld (field), -stein (stone), -bach (stream), -heim (home).
- **Examples:** Dunfaire (dún "fort" + faire "watch"), Sternbrunn (Stern "star" + Brunnen "well"), Weizenhof (Weizen "wheat" + Hof "farmstead"); The Oak & Lantern stays plain English. A happy accident: Egan, Hayes and Keegan all come from the Irish Aodh, "fire", fitting the flame god and his bloodlines.
- **Under review** (Oct 30, 2026): a redraft of the gods (only Morin, Tod and Egan fit the roots now; the gods may stay an exception), and of Gronogon and King Grel. Compared with the current names before anything changes.
- Town names: [characters → Places](characters/Places.md).

## Magic and the gods (lore)

- Magic is manifested through souls and the force of will behind them.
- Gods are great beings whose followers believe in them; that belief makes them strong. Gods are killable — they are just immensely strong because of their followers' faith.
- Wizards invoke magic using spell materials from animals or objects infused with soul energy. They grow stronger by harvesting more powerful spell materials. For now this is lore only, not a mechanic (it may become one later).
- Clerics invoke magic granted to them by the gods they follow. They grow more powerful through a two-way bond: their faith in their god, and their god's belief in them.
- Other casters: Bards, elves and tieflings cast with their own force of will. Arcane Knights and Arcane Tricksters cast through spell materials, like wizards. Rangers and druids channel the souls of the living plants and animals around them. Holy Defender fighters are the cleric-adjacent subclass, drawing on a god like clerics do.
- Fey have an overwhelming sense of self and draw their magic from themselves.
- The cast-out god is growing strong again because the cultists' faith feeds it.

## Pantheon

- 8 gods, plus Egan, the cast-out god.
- All eight gods cast Egan out together.
- The cast-out god, Egan, was the god of flame. Those who wish to burn the world down and start it anew are drawn to him.
- Each of the eight gods has a grand temple (the location of its god's holy land, a tier 3 region; see Grand temple quests). Churches also belong to the pantheon.
- How clerics and Holy Defenders choose gods, and how orders cut across gods: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Cleric spells.

**Tieflings and the flame god's bloodlines**

- Tieflings are descended from the cast-out god. Tiefling types are different bloodlines of the flame god — the Hayes, the Conleys and the Keegans — and each bloodline sets its spells and racial card (the race mechanics are in [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Races).
- **Three faces of flame** (decided Oct 27, 2026): each bloodline carries one face of Egan's fire. Hayes: destruction (burn it down). Conley: rebirth (start anew, which is why the cult wants their blood). Keegan: the hearth (a steady, controlled flame).
- Tieflings face an ingrained stigma, though people don't know why.
- The Hayes side fully with the cult and appear as recurring rivals: named family members the hero fights several times across the story (who they are: decided with the enemy roster).
- **Playable Hayes** (decided Oct 27, 2026): a player can be a Hayes who turned away from the cult. NPCs and the Hayes rivals react to it (how: open).
- The Keegans stay neutral and simply live with the stigma.
- The Conleys abandoned Leo, and the cult wants to use their bloodline.

## Main story outline

- Central threat: an eldritch force awakening. The force is a fallen god of the pantheon, cast out by the other gods.
- Structure: chapters, with no fixed count. Completing story quests advances to the next chapter.
- Each background's starting quest leads into a shared main story. The main story has an ending, and play continues afterward.
- Story beats arrive both as Story cards placed by the story at fixed locations and as events triggered in existing hexes (Story card rules: [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Card types; placement: [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Placement).
- Early game in the style of Octopath Traveler / Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave: each background has its own starting quest and its own opening chapters, which eventually converge into one shared path. The number of background chapters varies by background. (How this works in multiplayer: [Multiplayer & tech](06 Multiplayer & tech.md).)
- Every background's starting quest and opening chapters build up to the same single event at the point where the paths converge and the shared main story begins: a tear/rift in the sky, seen everywhere.
- The rift is opened when the Criminal path's client consumes the key to the god's prison. The client is the future avatar. In every other path this happens off-screen: those heroes only know something is wrong, which the cultist appearances play up. Only the Criminal route reveals why the rift opened.
- After the omen, the rift stays visible in the sky and grows each chapter.
- Tone varies by region.
- Cultists of the force start appearing as encounters; much later, an avatar of the force appears.
- As chapters advance, the force's influence spreads across the map: cultist encounters and corrupted hexes start to appear (corruption rules: [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Corruption).
- The final fight with the avatar takes place on a Story card.
- Ending: the force is defeated.
- After the main story ends, play continues with remaining Legendaries and side quests, the chase to level 100, harder content farther out, and new main quest lines.

## Background paths

- Each background path follows the Hero's Journey and has its own mentor, who dies or leaves partway through, as in the Hero's Journey; how it happens varies per path.
- Each path offers 2 NPC companions to recruit, leaving the third companion slot open on purpose for NPCs from other paths. After the paths converge, the player can meet the NPCs from the other paths at their home locations.
- Every path NPC has a unique personality and role.
- All routes converge on Gronogon, a named city Story card.
- Each path's companions are balanced around the classes most likely to take that background, but any class can take any background.
- Each companion has a default subclass (listed in its cast table), which the player can change when it unlocks at level 5.
- NPC companions' restriction, which keeps their power level distinct from the hero's: they get no class or subclass quests. Their NPC-related personal quests are their power-up instead.
- General companion rules: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Hero and companions.

## Shared main story (after Gronogon)

- All six paths converge on Gronogon. On arrival, every hero meets (or is met by) the king, King Grel, who asks or charges them to investigate the cultist plot. From there the main story plays out mostly the same for every background, with slight variances in how the hero responds to certain situations depending on their background.
- (The old Dungeon card reward on reaching Gronogon was dropped on Oct 24, 2026, since dungeons are now region landmarks.)
- King Grel is a trustworthy ally.
- Background variances appear as different dialogue lines, extra dialogue options, and different NPC reactions (how dialogue is written: see [characters → Writing guide](characters/00%20Writing%20guide.md)).
- First lead: climb the highest peak in the region (a Story card) to study the rift. The climb includes a cultist ambush, a hermit/NPC, and a low-level eldritch horror. On the mountain they learn the prison is a certain dungeon: the hermit, an elf who remembers the god, tells the hero where it is, and the prison dungeon is placed on the board as a fixed location.
- NPCs from the other paths are found at region locations (see NPC homes and stories).
- The elf hermit is a quest NPC only, not recruitable.
- The prison dungeon is a Story card, placed at a fixed location in unexplored territory; the player has to place cards to reach it.
- Inside, the party finds the god's empty cell.

## Quests

### Side quests

- Quests come from NPCs and story beats.
- Two kinds of side quest. Procedurally generated quests are simple (e.g. deliver this letter, clear a stronghold or dungeon) and reward Rare items, XP and gold. Hand-crafted quests reward Legendary items.
- Sources: NPCs in villages, towns and inns; quest boards; random hex events; and companions' personal quests.
- Rewards can include gear/loot, XP and gold. Map cards are no longer quest rewards (Oct 24, 2026).
- No set limit on active side quests.
- Companions' personal quests unlock new action cards and unique gear for that companion (an exception to new cards coming only from level-ups, confirmed Oct 27, 2026: see [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Class action cards).
- Some side quests can be failed, e.g. ones timed by in-game days.
- **Quests target regions** (decided Oct 24, 2026): instead of handing over location cards, quests target the player's own regions and landmarks, e.g. "build a 4+ swamp" or "clear the landmark in your fey wood".
- Quest boards refresh after each rest.
- Generated quests scale with distance from the start, like enemies.
- Hand-crafted quests are mostly short chains of 2–4 steps. (Which Legendaries have no quest: see Other Legendary quests.)
- Multiplayer sharing of side quests: see [Multiplayer & tech](06 Multiplayer & tech.md).

### Minigames

Minigames are rare; most quests play out as combat. A minigame should be a game everyone already knows. So far: the Tactician's chess puzzle, the Gambler's poker game (played with real playing cards, not coins) and the Musician's Simon game (see [Cards & progression](05 Cards & progression.md) → Subclass quests).

### How hand-crafted quests play out on the map

Changed Oct 24, 2026: players no longer receive location cards. Legendary quests start case by case and guarantee their tier 3 region (see Other Legendary quests); later steps appear as the quest places them on the map. Other hand-crafted quests target the player's regions and landmarks. Social encounters are resolved with dialogue choices plus coin checks (e.g. CHA).

Generally, a quest's start names the landmark or region it begins on. Some quests are instead auto-given by an event (e.g. Tod's, after a party wipe). A quest can have several possible starts; each is a chance for the quest to spawn there, not a guarantee.

**Legendary quests are shortcuts** (decided Oct 24, 2026). Most Legendaries are now tier 3 regions (see [Map & exploration](02 Map & exploration.md) → Tier 3 variants), which players can reach by working up through the tiers or by finding a quest that guarantees the region, like the class quests guarantee class variants. Quest starts are set case by case: an ordinary landmark event (a regular landmark offers the quest), or the Legendary location itself (e.g. Lady of the Lake starts the Sword in the Stone quest).

**Example: Sword in the Stone** (confirmed Oct 24, 2026)

1. A river/lake region becomes an enchanted lake (tier 3), whose location is Lady of the Lake. Meeting her starts the quest.
2. A social encounter with the water maiden: she tells the player where the sword is, and the quest places Sword in the Stone on the map.
3. Optionally, the player can win the Lady's blessing (ways to win it: The Lady of the Lake, below). Pulling the sword out is a STR coin check; the blessing adds coins to it. Without the blessing, the player can still try with brute force.
4. Reaching Sword in the Stone and pulling the sword out completes the quest. A failed pull locks the sword out for good, adding stakes and repercussions to the choice of whether to seek the blessing first. The pull plays out in the same zoomed-hex scene as the talk with the Lady.

**The sword pull scene** (decided Oct 28, 2026)

- **Options:** "Pull the sword" (STR + Athletics, so a proficient companion can chime in), "Read the runes on the stone" (a Lore check: a pass adds 1 coin to the pull; a fail loses nothing), and Leave to come back later.
- **The sword is Excalibur** (decided Oct 28, 2026), and the pull needs 2 heads.
- **Coin amounts** (decided Oct 28, 2026): the Lady's blessing adds 2 coins to the pull and her curse takes 2 away; the runes add 1. Balance risk, accepted: with a typical 4-coin puller and the 2-heads pull, a plain pull passes 69%, blessed 89% (94% with the runes) and cursed 25%. A failed pull loses the sword for good, so threatening her nearly costs the sword.
- **The pull's name shows its bonuses,** like upgraded cards: each bonus adds a "+" and the curse takes one away. Pull++ with the blessing and the runes; Pull+ with one of them; Pull with neither, or with the curse and one bonus; Pull− when cursed with no bonus.
- **Its text** (written Oct 28, 2026, picked by Ethan from drafts; narration, since nobody speaks here: see [characters → Writing guide](characters/00%20Writing%20guide.md)):

| Moment | Narration |
| --- | --- |
| Arriving | "The stone stands alone beneath an open sky, the blade sunk in it to the hilt. Runes circle its base, worn almost smooth." |
| Runes read | "The runes name the old kings, one after another, each hand the stone let go." |
| Runes not read | "The runes are too worn to read. Whatever they once said, they keep to themselves." |
| The pull passes | "Steel sings against stone, and the blade comes free. Excalibur is yours." |
| The pull fails | "You pull until your arms shake, and the stone does not give. The sword is lost to you." |

- **Anyone in the party can pull** (who flips: [Classes & combat](03 Classes & combat.md) → Checks outside combat), and the blessing and the runes help whoever pulls.

**The Lady of the Lake** (decided Oct 28, 2026)

- **Voice:** ancient and formal, like an Arthurian legend.
- **What she judges:** worthiness, meaning honour and truth.
- **Ways to win her blessing:**
  - **Persuade:** a light cost on a fail: she refuses her blessing.
  - **Intimidate:** a heavy cost on a fail: she fights back, which starts a fight, with water spirits (fey) rising from the lake beside her. If the party wins, she yields and tells them where the sword is, but curses it: the pull flips fewer coins. Then she sinks into the lake for good (decided Oct 28, 2026): she never speaks again, and that enchanted lake loses its resting blessing.
  - **Offer a deed** (no check): she asks the hero to bring her fallen champion's blade home to rest in her lake. Bringing it back wins the blessing.
- **Her champion and the deed** (decided Oct 28, 2026): Sir Bedivere was her sworn knight, who guarded the lake for her and rode out to face something he couldn't beat; she mourns him. He fell far from the lake, in a chimera's lair, which the quest places on the map far away (matching her line "He fell far from these waters"). The chimera still dens there with his blade. She never says his name: the player learns it from the blade they carry back, the item "Sir Bedivere's Blade". (In the legend, Bedivere is the knight who returned Excalibur to the Lady.)
- **The old kings** (decided Oct 28, 2026: left vague): a legend only. Their names are cut in the stone, but they have no set history.
