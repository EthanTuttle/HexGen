# Prototype art

Art for the Godot and Unity engine prototypes. The decisions behind it (terrains, edge wedges, units, who does what) are in `design/06 Multiplayer & tech.md` → Engine prototype; scale and hex orientation are in `design/07 Presentation.md` → Art direction. Binary files (`.blend`, `.glb`, `.png`) go through Git LFS, so install it before pulling.

| Folder | What's in it |
| --- | --- |
| `scripts/` | Blender Python that builds the first drafts, exports and renders previews; `water.py`, `ground_cover.py`, `landmark_flow.py` and `fence_gates.py` are references the engines copy |
| `tiles/` | Map tile kit, `tiles_vN.blend` |
| `combat/` | Combat cell kit, `combat_cells_vN.blend` |
| `minis/` | Minis (worked on in its own session); `minis/standins/` holds the low-poly stand-ins |
| `export/` | `.glb` files the engines import; always the latest version |
| `preview/` | Rendered test views, `*_vN.png` |

## Rules for the art session

New art assets are made in their own **Art Planning** session (set up Nov 1, 2026). These rules apply to that session, and to anyone else adding art.

- **The docs decide the look.** Build from the design docs: tier 1 and tier 2 variant looks are in `design/07 Presentation.md` → Art direction → Region variant looks, and the variants and their landmarks are in `design/02 Map & exploration.md`. Don't invent looks, names or rules. Where the docs leave a choice open, give Ethan 3–4 options with the question tool, or list them in the report.
- **Hand-off goes through the planner session.** Report finished work to the session that started you, never to the Godot session directly. The planner shows Ethan the previews and passes approved kits to Godot.
- **Ethan reviews previews before the engine sees them.** Each group gets Blender preview renders: the region-finished map (`render_preview.py -- --variants`, or a map staged for the group) and a close-up of each variant. Nothing counts as done until he approves it.
- **Never overwrite.** Each change builds the next `tiles_vN.blend` and `preview/*_vN.png`. Older versions stay for reference. Add a line to **Versions** below for each new kit, and a section under **Units and pieces** for each new kind of piece.
- **Keep the piece contract.** A variant has `<variant>_centre`, `<variant>_edge_same` and `<variant>_edge_other`, registered in `scripts/variants.py` (`VARIANTS`). It uses its terrain's base with the top recoloured, picks edge pieces by terrain (`terrain_of`), and keeps origins at the tile centre on the ground, with edge pieces facing +X. Pieces must join seamlessly with every neighbour, including other variants of the same terrain.
- **Blend in, no rim.** A finished region changes to its variant's look but keeps blending into the land round it (`design/07 Presentation.md` → Exploration screen → Cluster goals).
- **True scale.** Minis are about 1.3 tall on a base that fills one combat cell. Trees and props are sized against them, and mountains stay bigger than trees.
- **Built for phones.** Use the shared `kit` material with vertex colours. Give a part its own material (`OWN_MATERIAL` in `scripts/hexkit.py`) only when the engine has to light or move it, e.g. anything that glows at night, like the lanterns. Keep triangle counts in line with tier 1, and report the triangles and materials per variant.
- **Effects belong to the engine.** Things that move, flash or shimmer (wisps, lightning, lava pulses, heat haze, swaying grass) are built in Godot. Build the props and the materials they need, and list each effect in the report for the planner to pass on.
- **World-space systems are shared references.** Ground cover, sand and water (`ground_cover.py`, `sand.py`, `water.py`) are ported by the engines. If a variant needs a change there, make it in the reference script and flag it in the report.
- **Git:** follow `CLAUDE.md`: your own branch and worktree, small commits, Git LFS for binaries, and merge to `main` when a group is approved.
- **Report:** the kit version, the previews, triangles and materials per variant, effects for the engine, reference-script changes, and anything left open.

## Refining a kit by hand

1. Open the latest `tiles_vN.blend` and **Save As** the next version first (`tiles_v2.blend`), so earlier versions stay for reference.
2. Edit the pieces. Keep each piece's name, keep its origin at the tile centre at ground level, and keep edge pieces facing +X.
3. Run the export on your new version. The engines pick up `art/export/tiles.glb` without any other change.

## Units and pieces

1 unit = 1 metre; Z is up in Blender, and the export turns it Y-up for both engines.

- **Map tile:** pointy-top, 9.0 tall corner to corner, 7.79 wide. Each terrain has four pieces:
  - `base_<terrain>`: the slab, with its ground on top at z = 0 and soil sides down to −0.6. The top is subdivided so the engine's shader can fade the ground colour toward a neighbour's.
  - `<terrain>_centre`: the middle of the tile, inside an inner hexagon reaching 45% of the way to each edge.
  - `<terrain>_edge_same` and `<terrain>_edge_other`: the wedge between the centre and edge 0, which faces +X.
  - `river_corner` (tiles_v2 only): water filling the corner between edge 0 and edge 1. Superseded: water will be drawn by the engines (see Versions).
- **How the engines assemble a tile:** place the base and the centre. For each edge i (0 = +X, counting anticlockwise in 60° steps), place `edge_same` if the neighbour there has the same terrain, otherwise `edge_other`, rotated by 60° × i. Then, for a terrain with a corner piece, for each corner i (between edge i and edge i + 1), place the corner rotated by 60° × i if both of those neighbours have the same terrain. A chain of river tiles stays a narrow river; a clump opens into a lake.
- **Linked terrains and borders (from tiles_v4):** farmland and village count as the same terrain when choosing edge pieces, so a farm track meets a village street. Both have a border on their `edge_other` pieces at the same depth, corner to corner. For each joined edge i, if edge i + 1 isn't joined place `border_join_l`, and if edge i − 1 isn't joined place `border_join_r` (both rotated 60° × i): they carry the border out to the shared edge, where it meets the matching join on the next tile. `BORDER_STYLE` in `scripts/build_tiles.py` picks fence (default), block wall or stacked flat-stone wall. Axial neighbour offsets per edge are in `scripts/hexkit.py` (`NEIGHBOURS`), and `scripts/render_preview.py` does exactly this.
- **Combat cell:** flat-top, spaced 1.0 across the flats, drawn at 0.94 so a thin gap shows. A radius-4 board (61 cells) has a pointy-top outline the size of one map tile. Pieces: `cell_grass_a/b/c`, `cell_tree` (doesn't block), `cell_rock` (blocks).
- **Minis:** about 1.3 tall, on a base that fills one combat cell.
- **Stand-in minis** (`standin_minis_vN.blend`, exported as `export/standin_minis.glb`): super low-poly blockouts (70–290 triangles each) so the engines can test size and movement before the real minis exist. Each stands on a flat-top wooden hex base 0.9 across the flats and 0.08 tall, with a coloured ring and a notch on its front edge; the pivot is the base centre at ground level. They face −Y in Blender, which exports as +Z (glTF front, Unity forward, Godot `MODEL_FRONT`). Pieces: `standin_base` (no figure), `standin_hero` (1.3, sword raised), `standin_chelmsford` (1.3, snout, horns, tail, mace and shield), `standin_small` (1.0, halfling or small elf), `standin_fluffy` (the cat on all fours, about 0.6) and `standin_enemy` (1.3, spear, red ring). Only the 1.3 height comes from the docs; the other heights and the ring colours are test guesses.
- **Enemy minis (`minis/enemies/enemy_minis_vN.blend`, `scripts/build_enemy_minis.py`; not exported until approved):** scripted low-poly, on the stand-ins' hex base (68 triangles of each count, pivot and facing the same), in the shared `kit` material. Pieces: `enemy_wolf`, `enemy_wolf_elite`, `enemy_spider`, `enemy_spider_elite`. An elite has a gold line inside the red ring. All are drafts for Ethan to judge.
- **Giant minis (`minis/enemies/giant_minis_vN.blend`, `scripts/build_giant_minis.py`; a look test, not exported):** the plain wolf and spider resized onto bases shaped to 2, 3 or 7 cells. The pivot is the footprint's middle, so an edge, a corner or a cell centre; a `footprint` property lists the cells. Grid rules are open (`design/03 Classes & combat.md`).
- **Forest combat cells (`combat/combat_cells_vN.blend`, `scripts/build_forest_cells.py`; not exported until approved):** per variant, flat open cells (`cell_<variant>_ground_a/b/c`, `_litter`), a darker `_cover` cell, and a stony `_block_a/b` cell filled by a mass 0.75 tall at most. Slim `tree_<variant>_*` pieces stand on cell corners, where a base can't cut them: every second corner of a cover cell, each corner once. `dark_wood_web` hangs between two trees one cell apart, `fey_wood_ring` goes round seven cells, and `board_under` is a tile-sized soil plate that shows in the gaps. Trees stand 1.25–1.9, about half the map tile's; `tree_<variant>_tall_*` (from v5) stand 2.5–3.5, map height, slimmer than the tile's so they fit a cell corner. `board_plate` (from combat_cells_v3) follows the outline of the 61 cells, for a separate combat map. From v4, moss on a rock is the colour of its upward faces, not a cap. The first draft's cells stay in the file. Rocks and brambles look blocked and trees look like cover, but what blocks or gives cover is still open.
- **Forest obstacles (from combat_cells_v6):** full-bodied pieces sitting on whole cells, each with its cells in the `footprint` property: `ob_<variant>_tree_a/b/c`, `_grove_2/3/7`, `_log_2/3`, `_stump`, `_boulder_3`, plus `ob_fey_wood_stone`, `_mushrooms_3`, `ob_dark_wood_hollow_3`, `_thicket_3`, `ob_heartwood_great_7`, `_roots_3`. `cell_<variant>_brush` is walkable cover and `cell_<variant>_floor` flat stony ground. Earlier corner trees (`tree_<variant>_*`) stay in the file but the maps no longer use them.
- **Forest combat maps (`scripts/render_forest_maps.py`, samples for Ethan to judge):** twelve plans, three per forest variant, 34 to 114 cells in varied shapes, each written as a top-down text plan with its tactical idea beside it. Rock shelves, cliffs and the soil plate take any shape, so each map makes its own (`shaped_piece`, `fc.plate`), saved in `combat/combat_maps_vN.blend` with `-- --export`, which also writes `export/combat_maps.json`. Hand-off: `handoff/combat_forest_kit.md`. The script checks that every walkable cell can be reached and shows from at least 2 of 12 camera angles, one of them a 60 degree rotate step (`-- --check`: rays from points on each cell, at the Godot prototype's combat tilt of 52 degrees and distance 22, minis left out; an obstacle's own cells aren't checked). `-- --kit` renders the obstacles laid out. Previews: `preview/forest_map_<map>_vN.png`, a few `_turned`, sheets `preview/forest_maps_<variant>_vN.png`, `preview/forest_obstacles_vN.png`.

Terrains: plains, forest, farmland, river (river/lake) and village, plus (from tiles_v5) hills, swamp, desert and ruins. Colours are the mockup placeholders, set in one table (`PALETTE` in `scripts/hexkit.py`).

- **Hills (from tiles_v5):** a heightfield over the whole tile, cut into the usual centre and edge pieces. Heights depend only on how deep a point is along its edge's normal and how far across the wedge it sits, so pieces meet without gaps however they're turned: the centre is a mound, an `edge_other` wedge slopes down to ground level at the edge (corners are always at ground level), and an `edge_same` wedge adds a ridge that meets the neighbour's at the edge's middle, so joined hills read as a range. (tiles_v5 also had a terraced style, dropped in v6.)
- **Mountains (from tiles_v6):** joined hills share one bigger mountain, built from matching parts. A hills tile with no hills neighbours places `hills_peak` (its own peak, on its mound). For each corner i (between edges i and i + 1) where both neighbours are hills, it places `hills_mountain_corner` rotated 60° × i: the three tiles round that corner each draw a third of one mountain on it. For each other joined edge i whose two corners aren't shared by three hills tiles, it places `hills_mountain_edge` rotated 60° × i: the two tiles each draw half of one mountain on their shared edge. Each part's outline only wobbles in step with how its copies are turned, so the parts meet exactly. `render_preview.py` does this. Sizes (placeholders): peak 2.6 tall, edge mountain 3.4, corner mountain 4.3; trees are 1.6–2.4.
- **Desert (from tiles_v7):** the sand isn't in the tile pieces. Like ground cover, the engine shapes it in world space (reference: `scripts/sand.py`), so dunes carry on across joined desert tiles and every crest on the map lines up with one wind direction. It lies flat within 0.3 m of any edge facing other terrain and rises to full height by 2.2 m, measured in world space so neighbours agree. Each desert tile's surface is a grid whose edge samples sit where the neighbour's do, so there are no cracks. Cacti, rocks and dry bushes are scattered on the sand from a world-space hash. The look is rolling swells: long soft waves about 6.5 m apart and up to 0.55 m high (chosen over wind ripples, which tiles_v7's preview also tried). The `desert_edge_other` piece keeps dry scrub and cracked hardpan in the flat strip; `desert_centre` and `desert_edge_same` are empty. (tiles_v5–v6 had dunes in the pieces.)
- **Swamp (from tiles_v5):** murky pools with mud rims between grassy hummocks, reeds, lilies and dead trees. The `edge_same` piece holds half a pool on the edge's middle, and the neighbour holds the other half. The pools are plates in the kit (`bog_water`), for the engine's still-water shader; they aren't drawn like rivers.
- **Region variants (from tiles_v8, `scripts/variants.py`):** a finished region's tiles swap to its variant's pieces: `<variant>_centre`, `<variant>_edge_same`, `<variant>_edge_other`. Variants keep their terrain's edge shapes, and edge pieces are picked by the terrain (a variant tile next to its plain terrain counts as the same). Tier 1 variants: `deep_forest`, `meadows`, `highlands` (with `highlands_peak` / `_mountain_edge` / `_mountain_corner`), `windmill_country` (plus `windmill_country_centre_mill`, which the preview uses on about half the tiles by `hash01(q, r, 7) < 0.5`), `market_town`, `delta`, `bog`, `wasteland`, `excavation`. Three need engine code as well as pieces: **meadows**' wildflower drifts are ground cover (`ground_cover.py`), the **delta** is water drawn wider (`water.edge_widths`, only where both tiles are delta) with islands, boats, a jetty and crates (`add_water(..., delta=True)`), and the **wasteland**'s lower swells, cracked flats, buried ruins, bones and dead trees come from `sand.py`. The bog's mist and its toadstools' night glow are left to the engine.
- **Forest tier 2 and class variants (from tiles_v14, `scripts/variants.py`):** `fey_wood`, `dark_wood` and `heartwood`, built like the tier 1 variants on `base_forest` (ground colours `fey_wood`, `dark_wood`, `heartwood` in `PALETTE`). They keep the forest's edge rule: `edge_same` runs trees up to the edge, `edge_other` keeps a thinner fringe, so they join plain forest, deep forest and each other. Because every edge of a kind shows the same piece, props are spread from the inner end of a wedge to the outer, so the six copies don't line up into a ring.
  - **Fey wood:** the plain forest's trees, with every third tree (counted outward from the middle) a round one in teal, violet or rose. A big fairy ring (about 2 across) of lilac mushrooms fills an open clearing in the centre piece, which has no trees; the `edge_same` trees stand well back from it on every side (the camera turns freely, so any side can be the near one), and stray mushrooms dot `edge_other`. The caps keep their own material (`fey_mushroom` in `OWN_MATERIAL`) for the night glow. They're taller than the bog's toadstools, with pointed lilac caps (the bog's are round and yellow). The drifting motes of light are the engine's.
  - **Dark wood:** bare grey-black trees, no two alike: most lean and kink, with 4–5 pointed limbs and twigs; some are tall thin spires, a few are snapped stumps (1.4–2.8 tall before snapping). Orb webs hang here and there: about one tree in four has one strung between two limbs, and each piece strings one between its two nearest trunks at mid-height. Each web is a faint sagging sheet with radial strands and two rings of spiral over it, and a strand out to each anchor, all in their own see-through material (`web` in `OWN_MATERIAL`, blended at `WEB_ALPHA` 0.3, shown from both sides), so the strands read as lines on a veil. Thorny brambles (a rough dark mound with red-brown thorns) and scattered bones lie throughout; `edge_other` (the wood's edge) also has a gnawed deer carcass or more bones. The ground cover's tufts are dark grey-green (`tuft_dark_wood`) and it has no flowers (`TUFT` and `NO_FLOWERS` in `ground_cover.py`).
  - **Heartwood (from tiles_v22; design/07 changed Nov 1, 2026):** big broadleaf trees standing dense on mossy ground (three in the centre, two in each wedge, set differently in `edge_same` and `edge_same_b`, which the engine alternates per edge), green with a lime-gold clump or two in each crown, topping out under 2.6. No offerings: the cords of ribbons and lanterns hang only on the Tree of Life, so it stands out. The trail wedge, `heartwood_edge_trail` (see Flowing into the landmark), carries a path down a lane between the trees (from tiles_v23 the trees beside it stand back and a little lower), under a simple wooden gate in the torii style (`gate_wood`; chosen Nov 1, 2026, over a carved waypost and a lantern post, which tiles_v22 keeps). tiles_v18–v21's heartwood (glades with prayer-line cords) is superseded.
- **Ruins (from tiles_v5):** crumbled stone on grass: a paved square with column stumps and a toppled column in the centre; `edge_same` carries a paved street lined with broken walls to the edge; `edge_other` has a wall stub and rubble. The engine's grass ground cover grows through them.
- **Landmark tiles (from tiles_v19, `scripts/variants.py`):** a landmark replaces one tile of a finished region (design/02 → Landmarks; looks: design/07 → Landmark tiles; pieces: design/06 → Tile build). It's built like a variant, on its terrain's base with its variant's ground colour, and `variants.LANDMARKS` names the variant it belongs to. A landmark in `variants.LANDMARK_CENTRES` is a centre piece only, on a tile that keeps its variant's wedges and ground colour (`variants.wedges_of`). **Entrances face edge 0** (from tiles_v22): each landmark's entrance faces edge 0's midpoint (+X in the piece's own frame), with a short approach from the centre piece's edge-0 side to it: the Tree of Life's main approach (stepping stones between two lantern posts), the Hidden Shrine's front and slabs, the Fey Court's door and stair, the Spider Nest's cave mouth and furrow. On a trail edge, the trail wedge carries the approach on to the edge's midpoint. The engine turns the landmark's centre piece in 60° steps so edge 0 faces the edge where the longest trail arrives (`landmark_flow.turn`, from tiles_v23; design/06 → Tile build). The pieces built facing -Y are turned by `variants.face_edge0`.
  - **Tree of Life (heartwood):** `treeoflife_centre` is one giant heartwood broadleaf (tops out at 4.1, against a hill peak's 4.2): a thick trunk on a flared foot, five great limbs, a wide green crown with lime-gold clumps and a raised top, ribbons and lanterns (`lantern`, own material) under its rim. Six low branches reach toward the tile's corners, each with a cord of ribbons and small lanterns sagging down to a post by the corner. Twelve great roots, two toward each edge, snake out from buttresses at the trunk to the wedges' middle. The wedges depend on the neighbour: `treeoflife_edge_trail` (`treeoflife_edge_path` in tiles_v19–v20) where it's heartwood (a trodden earth path, `path_earth`, from the edge to the trunk, with stepping stones, between the two roots, which run on to the edge), and `treeoflife_edge_same` / `_edge_other` everywhere else (the roots dive back into the ground before the edge, with moss and a mossy stone). The heartwood neighbour uses `heartwood_edge_landmark` on that edge instead of its normal wedge: the two roots carry on from the edge and dive in, the path runs on to its glade, and a smaller tree stands to one side. The roots cross each edge at `ROOT_Y` (0.65) either side of the middle, at a set radius and height, so they meet exactly from both sides. So the paths follow however many heartwood neighbours the tile has: one path stub per heartwood edge, picked like any wedge. From tiles_v21 the trail rule below picks them (every heartwood neighbour's trail leads into the Tree).
  - **Hidden Shrine (deep forest, from tiles_v20):** `hiddenshrine_centre` is a small stone shrine in a clearing, facing edge 0 (+X) from tiles_v22 (-Y before) ringed by a few low pines: a worn two-step base, two pillars, a lintel and a pitched mossy roof over a niche with a weathered robed statue (`stone_statue`), arms folded, moss on its head. Roots climb over the base and up one pillar, with ivy (`ivy`) on the stones. The clearing stays open for the engine's shaft of light. It has its own wedges, `hiddenshrine_edge_same` / `_edge_other`: deep forest's, with the pines kept back from the clearing and shorter near it, because deep forest's own wedges run tall pines up to the centre and hide the shrine. A few old slabs lead from the centre piece's edge-0 side to its steps. Nothing spills onto the neighbours.
  - **Fey Court (fey wood, from tiles_v20):** `feycourt_centre` only; the tile keeps fey wood's wedges (`variants.LANDMARK_CENTRES`). A door (`fey_door`) faces edge 0 (+X; -Y before tiles_v22) with a glowing fanlight, six glowing windows (`window_glow`, own material, for the warm light inside) are set round the trunk with sills, and two round balconies on brackets have railings. A stair of six flat glowing mushroom caps (`fey_mushroom`) climbs straight out from the door toward edge 0 (it wound round the trunk before tiles_v22). A few fey mushrooms stand about.
  - **Spider Nest (dark wood, from tiles_v20; reworked in tiles_v22):** `spidernest_centre` only; the tile keeps dark wood's wedges. An outcrop of dark, wet-looking rock (`rock_wet`, `rock_wet_dark`, darker than the plains boulders) round a big black cave mouth (`cave_dark`) facing edge 0 (+X), with a dark patch of ground in front. Thick web curtains spill from the mouth's lip down onto the ground, two orb webs span it, and webs drape over the rocks' tops (`web`). Three cocoons (`cocoon`) hang from the lip on strands. A few webbed black dead trees stand behind and beside it. Its ground cover is dark wood's (`TUFT`, `NO_FLOWERS`).
- **Flowing into the landmark (from tiles_v21, for Ethan to judge; `scripts/landmark_flow.py`, `variants.py`):** each forest variant's tiles lead the eye to the region's landmark (design/07 → Landmark tiles). **The rule** (`landmark_flow.py`, plain Python for the engines): once a region finishes, a breadth-first walk from the landmark through the region's tiles gives each tile its step distance; each other tile's *toward* neighbour is a region neighbour one step closer (a hash of the tile, `hash01(q, r, 9)`, picks between ties). An edge is a **trail edge** when either tile's toward step crosses it; both tiles use their `<variant>_edge_trail` wedge there (the landmark tile uses its own trail wedge if it has one, else its variant's), so the trails branch together and all end at the landmark. A variant tile touching a landmark with its own landmark wedge (heartwood next to the Tree of Life) uses that instead. Tiles one step from the landmark use `<variant>_centre_near`. The landmark's centre piece turns in 60° steps so its entrance (edge 0) faces the edge where the trail from the farthest tile arrives (ties by `hash01(q, r, 10)`); its wedges stay on their own edges, so the entrance edge always gets the trail wedge, and nothing stands in front of it. The region and landmark are fixed once it finishes, so nothing moves as the map grows.
